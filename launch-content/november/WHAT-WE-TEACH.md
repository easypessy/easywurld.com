# What this account teaches, and whether it should have a face

Two questions, answered plainly. No need to know the build to read this.

---

## Part 1 — What we teach

The account teaches **one subject**, in four parts:

> **A small business loses money in the gaps around the sale — not in the product.**

The product is fine. The gap is what happens between "someone saw you" and "someone paid you."
Every post in November is about one of four gaps.

### The four pillars

| # | Pillar | The gap | The fix it teaches | How often |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Say what you sell** | A stranger cannot tell what you do in four seconds | One plain sentence, at the top, in words a customer already uses | ~1 in 3 |
| 2 | **Be findable** | People search, and you are not in the answer | A claimed Google listing, a correct pin, your area in the caption | ~1 in 5, mostly week 3 |
| 3 | **Answer fast and follow up** | They messaged, you replied late or never | Reply time, the day-three message, a tracker so nothing is forgotten | ~1 in 3 |
| 4 | **Price honestly** | The number arrives without a reason or a date | Price early, five lines per quote, a limit next to the price | ~1 in 5, mostly weeks 3–4 |

**Nothing else is taught.** Not branding, not logos, not "mindset", not ads. If a post is not
about one of those four gaps, it is entertainment, and it is doing a different job.

### The three voices (why the posts look so different)

| Voice | Share | Its only job | How you judge it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Entertain** | ~2 in 3 | Buy attention | Views, follows |
| **Teach** | ~1 in 4 | Earn trust, start conversations | Saves, messages |
| **Sell** | 1 in 10 | Make it easy for the people already decided | Packs sold |

Eleven posts a day is seven entertain, three teach, one sell. That is the whole mix.

### The four repeating shows

Every post is one of four shapes, which is why the account feels like a channel rather than a feed:

- **FIX THIS** — a real page or listing, one problem, three fixes. No insults.
- **ONE THING** — a single sentence you can repeat to somebody else tomorrow.
- **STEAL** — a script, a checklist, a formula. Something copyable.
- **HONEST AD** — what we sell, what it costs, and what it will not do.

Plus **TRUE STORY** (a real thing that happened), **STREET** (strangers answering a question),
and **WE SAID NO** (something we refused, and why).

### The one-sentence version

If you only remember one thing when you film tomorrow:

> **We find the small gap where a business loses customers, and show them how to close it, in
> public, with the price and the limit attached.**

### Day by day — what each day teaches

| Days | Theme | The gap it attacks |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Everything you were told | Say what you sell; the chat is the shop |
| 3–4 | Price is not the problem · Your page on a phone | Price with a reason; the page on a real phone |
| 5–6 | The customer who vanished · Nobody knows what you sell | Follow-up; clarity |
| 7 | The receipts | Week-one numbers, honestly |
| 8–13 | The follow-up week | What customers ask, quotes, cheap-is-expensive, the first ten seconds, selling without begging |
| 14 | The receipts | Fortnight numbers, honestly |
| 15–20 | Fix this Lagos | Be findable: teardowns, the local map, Google listings, words, too-broad offers, proof you already have |
| 21 | The receipts | Week-three numbers |
| 22–27 | The honest ad | Price honestly: who should not buy, what is inside, the bundle maths, the limit, the buyer, what we refuse to sell |
| 28 | The receipts | Week-four numbers |
| 29–30 | The month in numbers · What we learned | The whole system, handed over |

## Part 2 — Should the account have a face?

Short answer: **a face will not fix the carousels. The format will.** But a face can help, if it
is the right kind, and there are three kinds.

### First, why the carousels are probably not working

Not because they are faceless. Because most of our carousels are **documents**: five slides of
sentences, on a plain background, with no reason to swipe. A feed does not read documents.

The evidence is in our own month: the posts that travelled were teardowns (a real page, a real
problem) and street interviews (a real person, a real answer). The posts that got saved were
checklists. The posts that did nothing were the ones that looked like slides of a memo.

So the fix has two parts, and the second one is the one you asked about:

1. **Change the shape of the carousel** — start with a screenshot or a chat, not a title slide.
   One idea per slide, and the fourth slide is where the turn goes, so there is a reason to
   keep swiping.
2. **Give it a recurring cast**, which is what actually creates curiosity.

### The three kinds of face, and what each one costs

| Option | What it is | Cost | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. An invented person** — an AI-generated "founder" who appears to be a real human with a real life | Highest curiosity in the short run | High: you must keep one consistent face, voice and backstory across hundreds of posts, forever | **Disqualifying.** The moment anyone asks "who is this", you are maintaining a lie, and one lie makes every real number in this account suspect. It also breaks the rule this whole build runs on: nothing invented. **Do not do this.** |
| **B. A labelled AI narrator** — an obvious character that never pretends to be human | Medium curiosity, zero risk | Medium: needs a visual identity and a consistent voice | Low, but the "AI" label kills the mystery you are trying to create. Honest and slightly cold. |
| **C. A recurring cast of formats** — the characters are *your customers*, seen through their phone | The curiosity comes from "whose chat is this?" not "who is this person?" | Low: no face to keep consistent, no backstory to maintain | **None — because nothing is claimed.** |

**Recommendation: C, with a light version of B if you want a mascot later.**

### What C actually looks like

The account gets three recurring "characters" that are formats, not people:

- **The Chat** — a customer's phone screen. Recurring cast: the man who says "let me think about
  it", the customer who sends nine "hello?" messages, the woman who asks for a discount on the
  last day. Everyone recognises somebody.
- **The Replies** — a business posts something confident, and the replies take it apart. This is
  the X/Twitter reply format, and it is the most shareable thing on the internet right now.
- **The Thread** — a Reddit post ("Am I wrong for…") with comments. Strangers being blunt in
  public is funnier than anything a brand can write about itself.

None of these needs a face. All three create the "wait, is this about me?" feeling, which is the
actual engine of a share.

The full bank of 30 is in `MEME_BANK.md`, one per day, matched to that day's theme. It is
generated from `content/memes.json`, so it cannot drift from the calendar.

### The rule that keeps C honest

These memes are **jokes, not evidence.** Three rules, and they are not negotiable:

1. **Never post a chat meme as if it happened.** No "this happened today", no real dates, no
   real names. Generic names, exaggerated dialogue, obviously a bit.
2. **Never use a meme in a sales post, and never as proof.** The moment a joke becomes a
   testimonial, it is an invented testimonial.
3. **Never punch down** — not at the customer, not at a competitor, not at a country. The
   business in the meme is always the punchline, or nobody is.

If a meme cannot survive those three, it does not go out. There are 30 others.

### The two-week test, if you want to try the face properly

Do not redesign the account around a mascot on a hunch. Test it:

- **Week 1:** run the 30 memes in `MEME_BANK.md`. Same schedule, swap the Instagram carousel for
  the meme on that day's post. Measure saves and shares only.
- **Week 2:** keep whichever two formats had the highest shares-per-post, and repeat those.
- **Then decide.** If shares did not move, the problem was never the face, and you have saved
  yourself a year of maintaining a character.

That test costs nothing but the hour it takes to build seven images.
