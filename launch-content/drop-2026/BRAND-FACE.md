# The face of the brand — what to do about it

Short answer: **use a face, but do not pretend it is a person who does not exist.**
The three test images are in `face-A-woman.png`, `face-B-man.png` and `face-C-working.png`.

## What the images are

Generated portraits in the Easywurld palette (cream background, deep green and terracotta
clothing, natural window light, phone-camera framing). They are good enough to use on a
feed next to the type-only posts, and they solve a real problem: a feed that is all
cream rectangles with serif type looks like a template pack. A face makes an account
look like a person sells from it.

## The risk, said plainly

Your whole content library argues for honesty — Day 04 is "cheap is an apology", Day 18
is "a broken site still looks fine", post 4 on most days is "what this pack will not
do". A synthetic founder contradicts that the first time someone asks "is that you?"
and a buyer has seen the same model on somebody else's page. Lagos buyers are good at
spotting stock, and the trust you spend is worth more than the photo.

Meta also now asks you to label photorealistic AI content in some placements. A brand
face that never gets labelled becomes a small compliance risk on top of the trust risk.

## The version that works

1. **Real face, real name.** Best option. You on camera for the talking-head beats
   ("9:47pm, still typing", the how-to demo). Phone, window, one take. The script is
   already written for every one of those — that is what the "Talking head" format in
   the plan is for. Nobody expects polish from a studio in Lagos; they expect a person.
2. **AI for scenes, not people.** Desks, hands, phones, notebooks, a shop counter, the
   close-up of a chat that went quiet. These carry the story without anyone claiming to
   be someone. Use `face-C-working.png` style frames as B-roll, cropped below the chin
   or shot from behind the shoulder.
3. **If you truly will not go on camera:** keep a face, but make it a character with a
   name and a stated nature — "the Easywurld desk" — and use the same image everywhere,
   so nobody thinks they are being introduced to a founder. Disclose it in the bio in
   one plain line. The brand stays warm, and no claim is false.

## What not to do

- Do not run a synthetic founder through a testimonial, a "when I met this client" story,
  or anything in the first person that implies a life that did not happen.
- Do not mix the three faces. One face, everywhere, or none.
- Do not let the face do the work the words should be doing. The words are already the
  strongest thing in this kit.

## If you choose the real face

Three shots cover the whole month, shot once on a phone:

| Shot | Use | Setup |
|---|---|---|
| Head and shoulders, calm, looking at camera | Profile, offer posts, story cover | Cream or plain wall, window on one side, phone at eye level |
| Working: phone in hand, mid-reply | Story beats, "the chat that went quiet" | Desk, notebook, plant. Do not stage it too neatly |
| Walking or turning, not looking at camera | Reels, hot takes | Same clothes as the other two, so the month looks like one person |

Shoot all three in the same twenty minutes, same shirt, same wall. That continuity is
what makes a feed look like a business instead of a stock library.
