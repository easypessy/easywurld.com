## 1. What was wrong before today

The 24 emails already in the Sent folder went to copywriters, content agencies,
business directories and news sites. That list cannot buy from you:

- **Copywriters and agencies** do the same work you do. They read your email as a rival
  pitching them. One or two may subcontract, but that is not a customer base.
- **Directories and news sites** (BizFinder, Yellow Pages, Lagos Business News, ASBON)
  have no budget for website copy. They sell listings.
- **Six addresses did not exist**, so a quarter of the effort produced nothing at all.

None of that was a copy problem. It was a list problem.

## 2. Who to write to instead

Firms that sell **trust and fees**, not products, and whose website is the first thing a
client sees before deciding to call:

1. **Law chambers** — Lagos is full of five-to-fifteen-lawyer firms.
2. **Chartered accountancy firms** — audit, tax, advisory. They sell reassurance.
3. **Architecture and interior practices** — their work is visual, their words are not.
4. **Clinics, dental practices, schools, estate developers** — same shape, bigger fees.

The pattern that makes someone a good target: **excellent at the work, weak at saying it.**
You are looking for a firm with a real address, real names, real photographs of real work —
and a website that reads like it was written by whoever had time.

**Do not write to:** other agencies, freelancers in your own trade, directories, "startups"
with no revenue, or anyone abroad whose timezone makes a phone call impossible.

## 3. The daily loop — 30 minutes, on the phone

**Minutes 0–10 · find twelve firms.** One search, one page of results:

- `Lagos law firm "info@" chambers` — then open five sites
- `Lagos chartered accountants "info@" audit tax`
- `Lagos architects "info@" Lekki`
- `Lagos dental clinic "info@" book appointment`

Rule: **only take a firm whose email address is printed on its own website.** Never guess
`info@` — a guessed address is a bounce, and bounces are what get an account flagged.
Check the domain in the address matches the domain you are on. Two of today's fourteen had
this wrong on their own site.

**Minutes 10–20 · find one visible defect per firm.** Not an opinion — something you can
point at in one sentence:

- two different founding years on the same page
- a contact address that is misspelled
- the best sentence on the page buried halfway down
- six services in one comma-separated paragraph
- a promise (free consultation, reply within a day) that is invisible from the homepage
- a credential that matters, lost in a list of letters

**Minutes 20–30 · send.** Four parts, under 160 words. That is the whole email.

## 4. The email skeleton

```
Subject: <the defect, said plainly — no "quick question">

Hi <name or team>,

<The defect. Quote their own words. One or two sentences. No insult.>

<Why it costs them. One sentence, about their client, not about marketing.>

<What you do. One sentence. No adjectives.>

<The free offer. "Reply and I will send you X, free." Then: "If not,
reply 'no' and I will not write again.">

Abu
Easywurld — Lagos
WhatsApp 0905 069 0837
<portfolio link>
```

**Rules that are not negotiable**

- Never claim a client, a result, a percentage or a testimonial you cannot show.
  The portfolio says *concept project* on every card. Say so if asked — it wins more
  arguments than it loses.
- One link, no attachments, no images, no tracking pixels.
- No "I hope this email finds you well". No "just following up". No exclamation marks.
- Never quote a price in the first email. The ask is a reply, not a sale.
- The free offer must be small enough that you will actually do it: three lines, one
  paragraph, one rewritten screen. Not "a free audit".
- If someone says no, they are out. Permanently. That is what keeps the account clean.

## 5. The follow-up sequence

| When | What | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Day 0 | The email above | under 160 words |
| Day 3 | Two lines: "Sending this once more in case it landed badly. The offer stands — three lines, free." | under 40 words |
| Day 10 | "Closing the file on this. If the site copy ever becomes a priority, you have my number." | under 30 words |
| After that | Nothing. Ever. | — |

Three touches, then out. A fourth is what makes people mark you as spam.

**Today's follow-ups are due 5 November** — the sheet has the date on every row.

## 6. When someone replies

**"Yes, send it."** Send the free thing **within the same working day**. Do the work
properly — this is the whole pitch. Then one line: *"That's the three lines. If you want
the rest of the page done, I charge a fixed fee per page. Want the number?"*

**"How much?"** Never answer with a number on the first reply. Answer with a question:

> "Depends which pages and how much is wrong with them. Two questions: is it just the
> homepage, or the service pages too — and who approves the words, you or a partner?"

Then quote one fixed price for one page. Small first job. The second job is the real one.

**"Not now."** *"Understood. I'll leave you alone — if it comes up later, you have my
number."* Then actually leave them alone. Put the row to `no` in the sheet.

**No reply.** Do not re-email the same firm in under three months. If they hire later they
will remember that you were the one who did not nag.

## 7. What to expect — the honest arithmetic

Cold email to professional firms in Lagos, sent from a personal Gmail, with no brand
recognition, converts at roughly:

- **1 to 3 replies per 100 delivered emails.** Today's 15 might produce nothing. That is
  normal, and it is not a signal to stop — it is a signal that 15 is too small to read.
- **100 delivered emails a week** (12 a day, five days) is a realistic ceiling before
  Gmail starts throttling. Expect **1 to 3 conversations a week**, and **one paid job a
  month** to begin with.
- The curve improves when the emails are specific. A generic "I help businesses grow
  online" gets zero. "Your contact address bounces" gets read to the end.

**What kills this fastest:** sending 200 in one day from a new account; attaching a PDF;
using a list you bought; and emailing the same person four times.

## 8. Fix these on the sending account this week

1. **Gmail display name is currently blank.** Every email today arrived from a bare
   address with no name attached. Gmail → Settings → Accounts → *Send mail as* → edit →
   name: `Abu — Easywurld`. Two minutes, and it changes how every email is read.
2. Add the signature (name, Easywurld, Lagos, WhatsApp) so it is not typed by hand.
3. When a job lands, buy a domain and send from `abu@easywurld.com`, keeping Gmail as the
   inbox. Cold email from a custom domain on its own IP warms up over about three weeks.
4. **Warm-up rule for a new address:** 5 emails a day, week one. 10 a day, week two.
   12–15 a day after that.
