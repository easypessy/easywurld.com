# THE FACE — read this before you post a face

You asked whether the brand should have an AI face. Short answer: **yes to a face, no to a
fake person.** Three test images are in this folder (`face-A-woman.png`, `face-B-man.png`,
`face-C-working.png`) so you can see what it would look like before deciding.

## Why a face helps

A feed of cream rectangles and serif type is beautiful and forgettable. A face makes an
account look like a person sells from it, which is what buyers on Instagram and TikTok
actually respond to. One consistent face across thirty days also does something no caption
can: it makes the account recognisable in a scroll.

## Why a synthetic face is risky here

Your whole library argues for honesty. Day 04 is "cheap is an apology". Day 18 is "a broken
site still looks fine". Post 4 on most days is literally "what this pack will not do".
A generated founder contradicts all of that the first time a buyer asks "is that you?" — and
Lagos buyers are very good at spotting a face they have seen on someone else's page.

Meta also asks you to label photorealistic AI content in some placements. A face that is
never labelled is a small compliance risk on top of the trust cost.

## The version that works

1. **Real face, real name — best.** You on camera for the talking-head beats. Every one of
   those scripts is already written; the format in the plan says "Talking head". Phone, one
   window, one take. Nobody expects polish. They expect a person.
2. **AI for scenes, not people.** Desks, hands, phones, notebooks, a shop counter, the close-up
   of a chat that went quiet. `face-C-working.png` is exactly this kind of frame — use it as
   B-roll, cropped below the chin or shot over the shoulder, never as "me".
3. **If you will not go on camera at all:** keep a face, but make it a character with a stated
   nature — "the Easywurld desk" — used identically everywhere, disclosed in the bio in one
   plain line. Then nothing you post is a false claim.

## What not to do

- Never put a synthetic face on a testimonial, a "when I met this client" story, or anything
  in the first person that implies a life that did not happen.
- Do not mix the three faces. One face everywhere, or none.
- Do not let the face carry a post the words cannot. The words are the strongest thing here.

## If you choose the real face

Three shots cover the whole month, taken in one twenty-minute session:

| Shot | Use | Setup |
|---|---|---|
| Head and shoulders, calm, looking at camera | Profile, offer posts, Story covers | Cream or plain wall, window on one side, phone at eye level |
| Working: phone in hand, mid-reply | Story beats, "the chat that went quiet" | Desk, notebook, plant. Do not stage it too neatly |
| Walking or turning, not looking at camera | Reels, hot takes | Same shirt as the other two |

Same clothes, same wall, same light. That continuity is what makes a month look like one
business instead of a stock library.

**Decision needed from you:** real face, scene-only AI, or a disclosed character. Nothing in
