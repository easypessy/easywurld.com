3. Why 37 posts a day is allowed, and where the risk is

Platform ceilings per 24 hours, from Buffer's current limits page: Facebook 35, Instagram 50, TikTok 25, LinkedIn 50. So 11 on each of your four platforms is inside the limits on all four.

The risk is not the platform blocking you. The risk is your own quality and your own attention.

  • TikTok is where 11 a day is cheapest, because the same idea becomes a new video

each time. Make 11 TikToks a day, you will burn out by day 9.

  • Instagram at 11 a day is fine mechanically, but a feed of 11 carousels in a row

reads as a spam account to a new visitor. Mix the formats.

  • Facebook at 11 a day is the most likely to annoy the people who actually buy from

you. Their reaction is not a ban, it is muting.

  • LinkedIn at 4 a day is close to the useful maximum. See below.

What we would do if this were our account. 11 core posts a day is a good idea bank, not necessarily a good publishing plan. Run the full 11 on TikTok, 7 on Instagram, 5 on Facebook, 4 on LinkedIn, and keep the other four ideas in the bank for the day you need them. Then watch which ones get replies. Volume is cheap, replies are the signal.

What this pack does. It gives you the full 38 slots a day, every one written, with the optional LinkedIn post marked as optional. It is the plan you asked for. If a day gets away from you, skip the slots you have to and use the app to see what is left, do not stack posts at midnight to catch up.

4. LinkedIn, specifically

  • The character limit for a post is 3,000. Every LinkedIn post in this pack fits.
  • LinkedIn publishes no fixed daily post cap, but the platform says it may limit posting

based on authenticity signals, and posting more than 3 to 5 a day is widely reported to cut reach. Same-day posts also compete with each other in the test window, because LinkedIn shows a post to a small slice of your network in the first 60 to 90 minutes.

  • One high-quality post a day is the practical sweet spot. Four a day is the outer edge

and should be used only for a week or two, not for months.

  • The 5th LinkedIn post in this pack is marked optional on purpose. Use it when you

have something genuinely worth a second slot that day, for example a big insight or a reply to a comment. Skip it otherwise.

5. What makes a post feel human

Research on writing that reads as human rather than generated converges on the same things, and they are all things you can control.

Do these

  1. Vary sentence length. Short punch, then a longer one that explains. If every

paragraph has the same shape, it reads as generated.

  1. Keep the specifics only you have. The customer who fixed your generator at 11pm.

The ₦18,000 Ankara set. The fact that you have been open since 2019. A draft that could apply to any business in your industry is missing this step.

  1. Lead with the actual point. No warm-up paragraph. First line should say the thing.
  2. Leave one trace of thinking. Say what you cut, what you would not claim, what you

would rather not do. A trade-off left visible reads like judgement.

  1. Use contractions and plain verbs. "You are" becomes "you're". No em dashes. No

"delve", "testament", "leverage", "unlock", "seamless", "robust", "game-changing".

  1. Ask one question at the end, or take one position. Not both.
  2. Let the rough edge show. A slight hesitation or a small admission makes it sound

like a person who has actually done this.

Never

  • Invented testimonials, client names, rankings, ratings, speed scores or results. You

cannot evidence them, and in 2026 people are more sceptical of AI-assisted content than they were two years ago. 69% of consumers say they are more sceptical of online content because of AI-generated material.

  • Fake scarcity, like a timer that resets. People notice.
  • Describing a demo project as client work. Every Easywurld portfolio name is a concept

project and a sample build, and it must be labelled as one.

  • Passing an estimated value off as a former price. Hours x ₦5,000 is an estimate of our

hours, and it is labelled that way every time.

6. The emotions to aim for, and where each one belongs

Emotional content performs about twice as well as purely rational content, and emotion is what earns attention, while clarity is what removes friction and closes the decision. In practice: emotion gets the scroll to stop, clarity gets the sale.

7. Memes as brand communication

How many: 2 a day, roughly 20% of output. That is inside the range where brands get reach without spending their credibility.

The rule that makes memes work: the meme has to live in the overlap between what your audience finds funny and what your business is about. A meme about the work of running a small business in Lagos, for a studio that helps small businesses get marketing clarity, is in the overlap. A meme about a completely unrelated internet trend is not.

Three formats that work for this brand:

  1. The problem meme. Joke about the exact frustration the offer solves, without

mentioning the offer. "Customer: how much? Me: ₦15,000. Customer: okay thanks. Me: seen at 2:14am". The reader laughs, then a few days later they see the offer post and connect it themselves. That is much stronger than a meme that sells.

  1. The industry insider meme. Jokes only people in your market would understand. This

builds an in-group feeling, and in-group feeling is what makes people reply.

  1. The founder meme. You are a small studio, and the business you are in is a

workshop with a deadline. Self-deprecating humour about your own situation lands better on a business audience than anything polished.

Never: political jokes, anything that punches down at a customer or a competitor, anything that would embarrass a customer, and any meme you had to think very hard about. If a joke is not obvious in two seconds, it is not a meme, it is a caption.

Do not put a logo on the meme. Let the humour carry it, and put the brand in the handle. The only branded element worth adding is the colour, so the grid still looks like one account.

Cadence: one meme in the morning slot, one in the evening slot. A meme at 9am is context, a meme at 9pm is the share.

8. Working from the phone, without it filling up

The problem you described. Downloading the full post pack with every image and video fills the phone, and then the folder is confusing: which file goes with which post, which day is today, what did you already post.

The fix, in three parts:

  1. Keep text on the phone, media in the cloud. The post copy, scripts and schedule are

text. Text is cheap. Images and video are what fill a phone.

  1. One file, one purpose. EASYWURLD_POST_COMMANDER.html is a single 1 MB file that

opens in a phone browser with no internet. Add it to the home screen and it behaves like an app. Every post, every platform version, with a copy button and a tick mark. It saves your progress on the phone, and you can export a backup file to Drive.

  1. Media lives in Google Drive. The Easywurld folder in Drive holds the images, the

videos, the offers PDF and the calendar. Open a file from Drive when you need it, use it, and let Drive's cache handle it. Your phone's storage does not need a copy.

The Sunday night batch. 20 minutes, once a week. Open the app, copy what you can, set the schedule in each app, and leave open the two slots that need a real human reply. Do not schedule a post you have not read out loud once.

The month is already dated, so the batch is bigger but the thinking is not. On the first Sunday of the month you set the whole month in one sitting, because every post already carries its date and time. From then on it is twenty minutes a week.

9. The weekly review, 10 minutes on a Friday

Open the app, Month tab. You get the posted count for each day and the total, and the grid shows you where the month is still empty.

Look at three things, and only these three:

  1. Which post got the most replies. Not likes. Replies mean somebody found you useful

enough to talk to.

  1. Which format worked. Carousel, reel, text. Double down on the one that replied.
  2. Which post got nothing. Do not post that type again for a week.

Write one sentence. Put it in the notes field of the app. Next week's first post should be based on it.

And once a month, on the last Friday, do the bigger version. 20 minutes. What got a message, what got a reply, what got nothing, and what you are dropping. That review is what makes month two easier than month one, instead of month two being a fresh start.

10. The rules that keep the account safe

  • No client results, rankings, ratings, testimonials or guarantees you cannot evidence.
  • Every demo project is labelled concept project · sample build.
  • Value figures are hours x ₦5,000, described as an estimate, never as a previous price.
  • Each honest post says plainly what the pack does not do.
  • Reply to people faster than you post to them. A reply to a real customer beats one more

post, and it is also the thing that shows up in the comments, which is what pushes the post further.

  • If you miss a slot, skip it. Do not stack three posts at 11pm to catch up.
