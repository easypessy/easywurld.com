### Instagram · 09:30 (6 slides, 4:5)

1.
> 3 things I didn't expect to learn
> in my first 3 weeks of Easywurld:

2.
> **Lesson 1: People don't buy the cheapest thing. They buy the thing they understand fastest.**
>
> I spent a long time worrying about price. Should it be ₦3,000? ₦5,000? ₦7,500? Would more people buy if it was cheaper?
>
> What I observed was that price almost never decided the sale. What decided the sale was whether someone could read the title and immediately see themselves in the problem. The Chat-to-Customer Playbook ("10 ready-to-send reply scripts") is the best-selling single product because nobody has to guess what it does or who it's for. Clear beats clever, always.

3.
> **Lesson 2: The sale is almost never in the post. The sale is in the reply.**
>
> I put huge care into the posts, but posts get a person interested — they rarely close the sale. The sale closes when someone messages "tell me more" at 9:23pm on a Tuesday and you reply like a real human being — not a sales script — answering the specific thing they asked, using their name, referencing the specific thing they mentioned in their message.
>
> If you're posting well but nobody buys, look at your DMs. That's almost always where the problem is.

4.
> **Lesson 3: Tired founders buy tired. Tired founders also sell tired.**
>
> This is the most important one. I had several customer conversations go badly in week 2 because I replied at 9:45pm when I was exhausted. I sent short replies. I was defensive. I missed questions.
>
> Once I instituted the 8pm hard stop — close the laptop, reply in the morning — those conversations all closed well the next day. Every single one. The customer was still there. They were happy to wait. A kind, clear reply in the morning beats a fast, grumpy reply at night, 100% of the time.
>
> This applies to you too. If you're reading this and replying to customers late at night because you feel you have to — you don't. Your customers will wait. Set the away message. Reply in the morning.

5.
> **A bonus fourth thing I learned: people buy from people who admit they are tired.**
>
> The posts that got the most replies and the most sales were not the polished "expert" posts. They were the honest ones — the Saturday behind-the-scenes, the post about napping on the couch, the post about snapping at my husband, the post admitting I had no idea what I was doing in week 1.
>
> People are tired of experts. They want real people who are also tired and building things anyway.
>
> That, more than any product, is what Easywurld is actually selling.

6.
> All nine products are ₦5,000.
> The bundles are ₦12,000 and ₦35,000.
> Say what the thing does in plain language.
> Reply to every message like a human.
> Close at 8pm. Rest.
> That's it. That's the whole strategy.

> what I learn, including the parts that don't work.
