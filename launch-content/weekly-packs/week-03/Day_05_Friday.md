## 🕣 08:45 — Post 02: When to ask for a referral

**Core idea:** There are exactly three right moments to ask for a referral, and a hundred wrong moments. Right after a positive moment — never during a problem, never when they owe you money, never randomly in a broadcast.
**Emotion:** Clarity.
**Objective:** Education.

### TikTok · 08:45 (5 slides, 9:16)

1.
> There are exactly 3 right moments
> to ask for a referral. Every other moment
> is the wrong one.

2.
> MOMENT 1: Right after they say thank you
> or tell you how happy they are.
> "So glad you love it! Who else do you know
> who might need something like this?"

3.
> MOMENT 2: When they come back
> for the second time and you know
> they're a happy repeat customer.
> "Thank you for coming back — it really
> means a lot. Do you have any friends
> who've been looking for this kind of thing?"

> "Thanks for being patient while I sorted that.
> Honestly if you know anyone else who needs
> this, I'd really appreciate you sending them
> my way."

5.
> Never ask:
> · When they are complaining
> · When they haven't received what they paid for
> · In a bulk broadcast message
> · Before they have even used the product
>
> Time the ask right and it doesn't feel awkward.
> It feels like a natural end to a good experience.

1.

> Asking for a referral feels awkward because most people ask at the wrong time. They ask in a random broadcast, or before the customer has even used the product, or when the customer is mid-conversation about a problem.
>
> Timed correctly, a referral ask doesn't feel like a favour. It feels like a natural end to a good experience.

3.
> **Moment 1 — Right after they say "thank you" or express happiness**
> This is the most natural moment in the world. They have just told you they love what you did. Smile and say:
>
> "That means so much — thank you. Who else do you know who might need something like this? I'd really love to help them too."
>
> Timing it here makes the ask feel like a continuation of the good feeling, not a pivot.

4.
> **Moment 2 — When they come back for the second or third time**
> A repeat customer has already proven they like you. When they come back, after greeting them warmly:
>
> "Thank you for coming back again — it genuinely means so much to see a familiar face. Do you have any friends who've been looking for [what you do]? I'd love to look after them too."
>
> Repeat customers are your most enthusiastic referrers because they have evidence (their own repeat business) that you are good.

5.
> **Moment 3 — After you solved a problem well**
> Counter-intuitively, people refer most after a problem was well-handled — even more than after a perfect first experience. Someone who saw you fix an issue with honesty and speed becomes a stronger advocate than someone who had no issues at all.
>
> After the problem is resolved and they thank you for handling it well:
> "Thanks for being patient while I sorted that — I really appreciate it. Honestly, if you know anyone else who needs this kind of thing, I'd be really grateful if you sent them my way."

6.
> Moments to NEVER ask
> · When they are actively complaining
> · Before they have received or used what they paid for
> · In a bulk broadcast message sent to all customers
> · In the same message you're chasing them for payment
> · When you have not delivered on what you promised
>
> Time the ask right and it won't feel like begging.
> It will feel like a warm invitation to share something good.
