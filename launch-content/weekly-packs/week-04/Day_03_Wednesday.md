4.
> **How to use it correctly (rush-safe)**
> 1. Paste the prompt, the customer's message, and your blunt "what I want to say" into the AI.
> 2. Wait for the reply.
> 3. Read what it wrote OUT LOUD. If you stumble on a phrase, that phrase needs changing.
> 4. Add their name at the start ("Hi Amaka,").
> 5. Change 2-3 words to sound like you. If it says "I apologise for the inconvenience" and you would never say that — change it to "so sorry about that" or "ah sorry!" or whatever you actually say.
> 6. If you remember a personal detail (their child's name, what they ordered last time), add that one thing.
> 7. Send.

5.
> The whole process takes 90 seconds. The result will be a reply that is warmer and kinder than what you would have written tired, but still sounds like you because you edited it.
>
> **What NOT to do**
> · Don't skip reading it out loud. AI can write cringey things — "I appreciate your patience" is a phrase no real Nigerian small business owner has ever said out loud. Change it.
> · Don't let AI call them "Dear Esteemed Customer" or anything awful. If it does, regenerate.
> · Don't leave out your "blunt reply" part. That instruction is what grounds the AI in what you actually mean, not a generic "we value your custom" response.
> · Don't use this for deeply emotional or complicated messages (a customer is upset about something serious) — for those, put the phone down and reply in the morning when you're rested.

6.
> **Why the "blunt reply" step matters**
> A lot of AI prompts ask the AI to "be warm" without telling it what you actually want to say, and you get back a generic pleasant reply that doesn't answer the question. By giving AI your blunt honest reply underneath ("no, too late") you tell it what the answer is, and it just wraps the answer in warmth.
>
> It will not lie for you. It will not fake-promise. It will just deliver your real answer in a kind tone. That is the whole job.

> **Rules for using this prompt safely (these are non-negotiable)**

> 3. **Add one personal detail that AI cannot possibly know** — reference your child, a customer moment from yesterday, something funny that happened at the shop, anything real. This is the difference between a generic AI post and your post.
> 4. **If it sounds generic when you read it out loud, don't post it.** AI can write a "correct" post that nobody would actually read twice. If you're bored reading it, your audience will be bored too.
> 5. **Never, ever let AI write personal stories.** The Mama Adaeze posts, the napping on the couch posts, the posts about my family — those are mine. AI can't write those for you. If the post is personal, you write it, or you don't post it.

5.
> **Why the strict rules**
> It is easy to start using AI for every post. It is easy to look at a 5-slide AI draft and think "that's fine, let's go" and post it. And then three weeks later your entire feed sounds like every other AI-generated small-business account on Instagram and you can't remember why anyone followed you in the first place.
>
> People don't follow you because your posts are polished. They follow you because your posts are yours. AI posts should be a bridge across one tired day, not the highway you drive on all month.
