## 🕗 08:15 — LinkedIn Business 01 (Business diagnosis)

**Headline:** The most underrated skill in business writing is the ability to write a first sentence the reader cannot skip.

> Most business writing fails in the first sentence.
>
> Not because the idea is bad, but because the first sentence is warm-up. It is "I wanted to share a few thoughts on…" or "In today's rapidly evolving landscape…" or "As we approach the end of the quarter…"
>
> The reader does not care about your preamble. They are scrolling through dozens of pieces of content, emails and messages, and they decide in two seconds whether to give you the next ten.
>
> A first sentence that works does one of four things:
> · Names a specific problem the reader has
> · States something the reader quietly believes but rarely sees written
> · Puts a number to something they suspected but could not quantify
> · Tells a 5-second story they recognise
>
> Everything after that is explanation. But if the first sentence is a warm-up, the explanation never gets read.
>
> This is not a trick. It is respect for the reader's attention.
>
> Before you publish anything this week, read the first sentence and ask: would I keep reading this if I did not write it?
>
> #BusinessWriting #Marketing #Copywriting #Communication

## 🕥 10:30 — LinkedIn Business 02 (Marketing insight)

**Headline:** Three pricing psychology principles that actually work for small businesses in Nigeria — and one that does not.

> I am wary of "pricing psychology" advice because most of it was written for American e-commerce stores in 2014 and does not translate.
>
> That said, there are three principles I see working reliably in this market:
>
> **1. Silence after the price costs you money.** When someone asks "how much?" and you reply with only the number, the silence is what kills the deal. Not the number itself. Always add one line of context ("that includes delivery to Lagos, ready in 5 days") and one next-step question.
>
> **2. People buy relief, not savings.** "You will save ₦10,000" performs worse than "you will no longer have to reply the same message twenty times a day". A small business owner does not get excited about saving 10k. They get excited about not having a headache anymore.
>
> **3. Specific prices beat round numbers for considered purchases.** ₦4,999 works for pure impulse. For anything above ₦10,000, something specific like ₦47,500 reads as a calculated price rather than a guess, and it builds trust.
>
> **What does not work:** Fake anchors. "Was ₦50,000, now ₦25,000" when there was never a ₦50,000 price. Nigerian customers can smell this. It damages trust faster than it makes sales.
>
> Pricing is not psychology tricks. It is clarity about what something costs and what that cost includes.
