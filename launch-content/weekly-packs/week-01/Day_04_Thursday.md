## 🕗 08:15 — LinkedIn Business 01 (Business diagnosis)

**Headline:** The reason most small business marketing fails is not bad creative. It is no distribution strategy.

> I see this repeatedly: founders spend weeks perfecting a carousel, a website, a launch post, or a proposal. They hit publish. They sit back. Nothing happens. Then they conclude the creative was bad.
>
> Usually the creative was fine. The problem was distribution — there was no plan for how the thing that was built was actually going to reach people.
>
> Before you build anything — a website, a content plan, a new offer — answer three distribution questions first:
> 1. Who specifically is this for?
> 2. Where specifically does that group of people already pay attention?
> 3. What is the specific plan for putting this thing in front of them there?
>
> If you cannot answer those, the best creative in the world will sit in a quiet corner of the internet.
>
> Most "great products that nobody bought" were not great products that nobody wanted. They were great products nobody heard of because the founder spent all the time on the product and no time on how people were supposed to find it.
>
> Distribution is not a postscript. It is not "then we will post on Instagram". It is the first question, not the last.
>
> #MarketingStrategy #SmallBusiness #Distribution #Growth

## 🕥 10:30 — LinkedIn Business 02 (Marketing insight)

**Headline:** Three website mistakes that quietly cost Nigerian businesses money — and none of them are about design.

> I review small business websites almost every week. The problems that cost money are rarely design problems. They are clarity problems.
>
> **Mistake 1: The hero says nothing.**
> The first screen says something like "Welcome to XYZ" or "Delivering excellence since 2019". A visitor has three seconds to decide whether to stay, and the first screen gives them no answer to "what do you do, who for, and what do I do next?"
>
> **Mistake 2: No clear primary next step.**
> Every page has five different things you can do — call, WhatsApp, fill a form, read the blog, follow on Instagram. When there are five equally-weighted next steps, most visitors choose none. A good page has one primary next step and maybe one secondary.
>
> **Mistake 3: The site talks about the business, not the visitor.**
> The about page tells the founder's story in 600 words but never answers the visitor's question — "why should I trust this person with my problem?" The services page lists features but never connects them to a specific outcome.
>
> Design that looks "nice" but makes these three mistakes will not produce enquiries. Clarity will produce enquiries even on a site that looks simple.
>
> Fix the message first. The design supports it.
