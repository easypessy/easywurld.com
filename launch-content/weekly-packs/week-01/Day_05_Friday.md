**Headline:** The trust problem most small businesses don't know they have.

> I see the same trust gap on almost every small business website and social profile I review.
>
> The business makes claims that sound impressive but provides nothing a prospective customer can independently verify. Then, when the customer doesn't buy, the business concludes "Nigerians don't buy online" or "people don't support small businesses".

>
> Trust is built by things that are verifiable:
> · Specific details about how you work, including what you don't do
> · Demonstrations of the work being done (even on sample projects, clearly labelled)
> · A physical location they can find, or a phone number they can call
> · Pricing named up front (or at least a range)
> · Answers to the obvious questions before they are asked
> · Real people with real names, not generic "we"
>
> "We are the best" is not on this list. "Trusted by 500+ clients" is not on this list unless you can prove it. "Premium quality" is not on this list.
>
> Trust is not built by saying you are trustworthy. It is built by giving a stranger enough concrete things to look at that they can arrive at the conclusion themselves.

**Headline:** Five audits you can run on your own marketing this weekend — no tools, no consultant.

> If you want to know what is working and what is not, you do not need a consultant or expensive software. Run these five audits this weekend with a notebook and two hours.
>
> **1. The "we" audit.** Pull your last 10 posts. Count how many sentences start with "we". Count how many start with "you". If "we" wins by more than 2:1, you are talking about yourself more than the customer.
>
> **2. The first-line test.** Pull up your homepage. Read only the hero. Close the tab. Can you write down what the business does, who for and what to do next? If not, the hero is failing.
>
> **3. The enquiry conversion check.** Count last week's WhatsApp messages from new people. Count how many became paying customers. If that number is below 10%, the leak is in your replies and follow-up, not your reach.
>
> **4. The CTA audit.** Look at every post from the last two weeks. Does each one have a clear next action? Or do they all end with "follow for more" or nothing? Posts without a job are entertainment, not marketing.
>
> **5. The proof count.** Count how many posts in the last two weeks showed something (a demo, a sample, a process, an honest limitation) versus how many claimed something ("best", "premium", "quality"). If claims outnumber demonstrations 3:1, start showing instead of telling.
