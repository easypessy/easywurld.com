## 🕗 08:15 — LinkedIn Business 01

**Headline:** Two weeks of building in public: what I have learned so far.

> Fourteen days ago I launched Easywurld with one product and zero customers. Here is what I know now that I didn't know two weeks ago:
>
> **1. The sell posts are not where people buy.** They buy after reading three ordinary posts where I am not selling anything. Consistent honesty outperforms any sales pitch.
>
> **2. Mistakes are your best marketing content.** The post about accidentally sending an unedited AI reply generated more DMs and sales than five "expert" posts combined. Imperfection builds trust in a way authority cannot.
>
> **3. The highest ROI marketing activity is not content. It is following up.** Sending one 12-word message to a ghosted chat returns more revenue in 48 hours than a full week of new posts.
>
> **4. Specificity beats cleverness every single time.** A post that says "we sew dresses that fit first try" beats a post that says "we bring excellence to women's fashion" by a factor I can measure in actual sales.
>
> **5. People will pay for saved time, not saved money.** The Playbook and Prompt Desk sell because they save typing, not because they "grow your business".
>
> **6. Rest is a growth strategy.** On the days I work past 8pm, I make a mistake the following morning that costs more than the evening work earned.
>
> Two weeks is a small sample. But these patterns are already clear. More next week.
>

**Headline:** The 90-minute Sunday content prep — the exact system I use to never run out of posts during the week (most weeks).

> I don't write posts in the morning. I don't wake up inspired. I write my week's posts on Sunday evening in 90 minutes, and then I schedule them. Most weeks this works. When I don't do it, I miss posts.
>
> The system, in order:
>
> 1. Pick the week's single message (10 min). Not 7 messages. One idea I'm saying 11 ways.
> 2. Brainstorm 11 hooks (15 min) — one for each role: diagnosis, lesson, myth, proof, growth, tutorial, opinion, story, automation, offer, belief. Most are bad. 3-4 are good enough to use.
> 3. Design all 11 slide-1s in Canva (30 min) — same template, same palette, different hook text. This is the visual anchor for the week.
> 4. Write captions for the first 3 days fully (20 min). The rest I write the night before, but having slide 1s done means I always have a starting point.
> 5. Schedule the first 3 days into Meta Business Suite, TikTok scheduler, and LinkedIn (15 min).
>
> Total: 90 minutes on Sunday evening. A glass of water. No phone. One batch. Then I close the laptop for the night.
>
> Batching is not about being hyper-organised. It's about removing the 7am panic of "what do I post today?". When the posts are already scheduled, I can spend the morning on customers instead of on content.
