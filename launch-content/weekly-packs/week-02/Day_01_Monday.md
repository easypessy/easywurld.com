## 🕗 08:15 — LinkedIn Business 01 (Business diagnosis)

**Headline:** The price race is a marketing failure, not a customer preference.

> I hear the same complaint weekly from founders in Nigeria: "customers only care about price".
>
> In most cases, the customer is not choosing price — they are choosing price because no business has given them a reason to choose anything else.
>
> When every vendor of a service says "we do quality work at good prices" with no further specification, the buyer's rational move is to compare on the only axis that is different: the number.
>
> The business that names a specific buyer, names a specific problem they are trying to solve, and demonstrates one concrete difference in how they work removes price from the top of the comparison list. The price is still asked. But it is asked in a context of "is this worth it for me", not "which of these identical options is the cheapest".
>
> Positioning is not marketing fluff. It is the difference between a conversation about price and a conversation about fit.
>
> #Pricing #Positioning #SmallBusiness #Nigeria

**Core idea:** Price anchoring — why the number you say first matters, and how to present price so people compare you to the right thing (the value) instead of to your cheapest competitor.
**Emotion:** Curiosity + relief.
**Objective:** Education.

> 1. What they get (the specific product or service)
> 2. What is included (delivery, alterations, support, timeline)

## 🕥 10:30 — LinkedIn Business 02 (Marketing insight)

**Headline:** Positioning work is pricing work.

> There is an argument I have with founders regularly: they come to me asking for "pricing strategy" when they actually have a positioning problem.
>
> You do not need a clever pricing model. You do not need anchoring tricks or tiered bundles (though those can help later). You need to be able to answer one question clearly:
>
> "Who specifically is this for, and what specific problem does it solve that my competitor does not solve?"
>
> If you cannot answer that, no pricing framework in the world will help you. You will always be compared on price because that is the only information on the table.
>
> If you can answer that, pricing becomes easier:
> · You know who you are talking to
> · You know what they value
> · You know what your competition does not offer
> · You can price against the value of the solved problem instead of against the cheapest option
>
> The businesses I see charging well are not using magic pricing. They are using specific positioning.
>
> #Pricing #Positioning #Marketing #SmallBusiness
