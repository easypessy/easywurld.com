# The next 30 days: where you are, how you position, and how you get paid

Written for someone who is new to this and does not want to be flattered. Short sentences.
Nothing here assumes you have read anything else.

---

## 0. The five things, if you read nothing else

1. **The build is finished. The market has not seen it.** You have 330 posts, 990 pieces, a
   tracker and a meme bank. That is an asset, not a result. Nothing in this repo can tell me
   whether you have posted any of it, so everything below assumes **zero audience**.
2. **Awareness does not pay in 30 days.** It pays in 60–90. If you need money this month, it
   will not come from the content calendar. It will come from conversations you start by hand.
3. **So there are two tracks, not one.** A cash track (20 messages a day) and an awareness
   track (the posts). They share the same raw material, so the work overlaps.
4. **Your position is not "web designer". It is "the person who says in public what your
   customers are already thinking".** Everything you have built already fits that. Nothing
   else you could claim would fit it as well.
5. **The carousels are not failing because they are faceless.** They are failing because they
   are documents. That is fixable this week, and it is fixed by shape, not by a mascot.

## 2. Why the carousels are not landing

You already sensed this, and you are right. Here is the diagnosis, from your own month rather
than from a guru:

- The posts that travelled in the plan are **teardowns** (a real page, a real problem, three
  fixes) and **street interviews** (a real person, a real answer).
- The posts that got saved are **checklists**.
- The posts that do nothing are **slides of sentences on a plain background**.

A carousel that opens with a title slide is asking the feed to read a memo. Nobody swipes a
memo. The fix is not a face. The fix is the first frame:

| Instead of this | Do this |
|---|---|
| Slide 1: "5 things your website needs" | Slide 1: a screenshot of a real page with one thing circled in red |
| Slide 2: explanation | Slide 2: "this is why nobody messaged you" |
| Slide 5: "follow for more" | Slide 5: the one fix, in one sentence |

If you want the meme formats, they are built: `MEME_CARDS.html`, 30 of them, one per day,
already laid out as a chat, an X post with replies, or a Reddit thread. Screenshot and post.

### The Emily / Ava question, answered honestly

You sent me `@emily_reels_growth`. **I cannot open TikTok pages — they return 403 to me every
time, so I have not watched a single one of her videos and I will not pretend I have.**

What I can see from search indexes (unverified, treat as a hint not a fact): that handle looks
like a small UGC-creator account posting generic "how to go viral" and "best time to post" tips
with tens of likes per video. There is a larger separate account, `@emily.growth`, at roughly
50.5K followers.

This is the trap, and it is the same one with the Ava content you keep referencing:

> **Do not take structure from an account whose own numbers are small.**

A growth-tip account can explain hooks for years and still get 50 likes, because explaining
hooks is not the same skill as making something people want to watch. Take **mechanics** from
them if the mechanics are testable. Never take **strategy** from an account that is not winning
with it.

### Which of their advice is worth anything (ranked by how much it moves the number)

| Advice you will hear | Weight | What to do with it |
|---|---|---|
| Hook in the first 1–3 seconds | **Huge** | Already done: every hook is now ≤12 words, roughly 2 seconds |
| One idea per video | **Huge** | Already done: one job per post |
| Repeatable format over novelty | **Huge** | Already done: four shows |
| Post 3–5× a week minimum | **Large** | You have 11 slots a day planned — see §6 for the honest trade |

If a piece of advice cannot be tested in a week, it is not advice, it is a personality.

## 3. The position

### What we are

> **Easywurld reads Lagos business pages and says, in public, what is costing them customers.
> Then it fixes it for ₦5,000.**

One line for the bio:

> **I read your page and tell you the truth. ₦5,000 to fix it.**

### What we are not

- Not a web designer (a designer sells taste; you sell a diagnosis).
- Not a digital marketer (they sell reach; you sell the four places reach leaks out).
- Not an agency (agencies hide; you publish).

### Why this position and not another

1. **It is the only one your content already proves.** 330 posts, and the strongest are
   teardowns. A position you have to perform is a costume. A position you already are is free.
2. **It is cheap to demonstrate.** A teardown costs one screenshot and twenty minutes. A
   "brand strategy" position costs a portfolio you do not have.
3. **It is hard to copy.** Anybody can claim to be a designer. Very few people will publish
   what is wrong with a stranger's page, by name, kindly, every day.
4. **It converts into the product you already have.** The teardown ends with "here are three
   fixes" — and the pack is those three fixes, written out, for ₦5,000.

### The four gaps you sell against

Say these out loud until they are automatic. Every post and every message is one of them:

1. **Say what you sell** — a stranger cannot tell in four seconds.
2. **Be findable** — they search, you are not there.
3. **Answer and follow up** — they messaged, you replied late or never.
4. **Price honestly** — the number arrives with no reason and no date.

## 5. The money maths

Target: **₦30,000** in 30 days. In Nigeria that is data for months, transport, and a buffer.
It is deliberately a small number, because a small number you hit beats a big number you miss.

**Every number below is an assumption, not a forecast.** After 100 messages you will replace
them with your own.

| Line | Conservative | Middle | Good |
|---|---|---|---|
| Messages sent (20/day × 20 days) | 400 | 400 | 400 |
| Reply rate | 5% | 10% | 20% |
| Replies | 20 | 40 | 80 |
| Buy rate on replies | 10% | 15% | 20% |
| Sales | 2 | 6 | 16 |
| At ₦5,000 | **₦10,000** | **₦30,000** | **₦80,000** |

Read that table honestly: **the conservative column does not hit ₦30,000.** That is the real
risk and you should know it before you start, not on day 29. Three things move you up the table:

- **Personalise the message with a real finding.** A generic "hi, I do websites" gets 1–2%.
  "Your Google listing says closed but you're open — here's the three things I'd fix" gets 10%+.
- **Send a thing, not a pitch.** You have `Free_Sample_5_Chat_Scripts.pdf`. It costs you
  nothing and it is proof you are not a scammer.
- **Follow up once, on day three.** Most replies come from the second message.

**If you sell one page at ₦40,000**, you hit the target in a single sale. That is what to be
open to. Do not chase it on message one; offer the ₦5,000 pack, and let the person who says
"can you just do it" become the ₦40,000 job.

## 6. The 30 days, week by week

### Now → 31 October (prep, about 4 hours total)

1. **Pick one pack to push** for the whole month: **the Chat-to-Customer Playbook** (it is the
   one people ask about, and the free sample already exists).
2. **Build a list of 60 businesses** (see §8). Do not message yet.
3. **Write your three messages** (§7) into WhatsApp Quick Replies.
4. **Put the bio up:** "I read your page and tell you the truth. ₦5,000 to fix it."
5. **Make sending the free sample a two-tap action** on your phone.

### Week 1 (1–7 Nov) — 100 messages, prove the numbers

- **20 messages a day, five days.** Two 45-minute blocks: 9am and 7pm. That is it.
- Post **3 a day**, not 11: one teardown, one checklist, one honest ad. Batch-film Sunday.
- Log everything in the tracker: sent, replied, bought.
- **Friday: calculate your own reply rate.** That number replaces the table above.

### Week 2 (8–14 Nov) — follow up, first cash

- **Follow up every non-reply from week 1** with one useful thing, on day three.
- 20 new messages a day.
- Post the week-2 content that is already written (`days-08` to `days-14`).
- **Target: first two sales.** Two sales is not the money — it is proof the message works.

### Week 3 (15–21 Nov) — the teardown week does double duty

This is the week the calendar already calls **Fix this Lagos**. It is the best week of the month
for you, because the content *is* the outreach:

- Publish one teardown a day.
- **Send that same teardown to the business, privately, before you publish it.** "I made this
  about your page — do you want me to take it down, or fix it?" That is the highest-reply
  message that exists, because it is true and it is specific.
- Ask every buyer so far for **one sentence** about why they bought. That is your first
  testimonial, and it is the thing you cannot manufacture.

### Week 4 (22–28 Nov) — convert the warm ones

- Everyone who replied but did not buy gets **one** message: "the price is still ₦5,000, and
  here is what it will not do" (the honest limit closes more than urgency).
- Offer the bundle at ₦35,000 only to people who already bought one pack.
- Keep posting. Keep 10 messages a day going.

### 29–30 Nov — count, and decide December

Fill in the sheet, then answer three questions: what was my reply rate, what was my close rate,
which pillar did buyers actually care about. That decides December's offer.

## 7. The three messages

Copy these. Change the bracketed parts. Never send a message that could have been sent to
anybody else.

**Message 1 — the finding (send this one)**

> Good afternoon [name]. I was looking at [business] on Google and noticed [one specific thing:
> the listing says closed / there is no price anywhere / the number leads to a different
> business]. I fix pages for small Lagos businesses, so I wrote down the three things I would
> change — want me to send them? No charge, no pitch.

**Message 2 — the three points (only if they say yes)**

> Here they are, in order of what is costing you most:
> 1. [specific]
> 2. [specific]
> 3. [specific]
> I have a ₦5,000 pack that does numbers 1 and 2 properly, with the words to copy. If it is not
> useful, tell me and I will stop. Here is a free sample so you can see how I write: [PDF]

**Message 3 — the follow-up (day three, once)**

> Following up once, then I will leave you alone. The offer is ₦5,000 and it will not bring you
> new customers — it stops you losing the ones already messaging you. If now is not the time,
> say so and I will check back in January.

**What never to send:** "Dear sir/madam, we are a leading digital agency…". That message is why
cold DMs have a bad name.

## 8. Who to message (the list is the hard part)

Build 60 names in one sitting. Three sources, in order of quality:

1. **Google Maps, filtered for broken listings** — search "tailors in Yaba", "bakeries in
   Lekki", "laundry Ikeja". Open the first twenty. You are looking for: no photos, wrong hours,
   a number that leads somewhere else, no website. **These businesses have a visible problem
   and a public phone number.** That is the whole qualification.
2. **Instagram, filtered for "DM for price"** — search a local hashtag, find posts where the
   seller replies "inbox me" to every comment. You already have a post about exactly this.
3. **Businesses you can physically walk past** — a shop with a sign and no price on it is a
   prospect. You are in Lagos; this is free.

Write down for each: name, what is broken, the phone number. That spreadsheet is worth more
than the calendar this month.
