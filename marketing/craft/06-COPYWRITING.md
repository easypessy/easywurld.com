# 06 — Copywriting

*Your fourth entry, and the one that connects the others. A hook is copy. A caption is copy. The
offer, the DM, the landing page, the follow-up — all copy. This file is the standard they get
judged against.*

---

## 1. Start with the thing you said

> **"We have to start loving our viewers."**

That sentence is not decoration. It is the operating rule, and it is testable. Because most
marketing writing is not hostile — it is **indifferent**. It talks about the seller, it assumes the
reader is stupid, and it asks before it gives.

Copy that loves the reader does five things, and you can check every one:

| What care looks like | What indifference looks like |
|---|---|
| Gives them something usable even if they never buy | Everything leads to a purchase |
| Says who it is **not** for | Tries to be for everybody |
| Puts the price where they can see it | Hides the cost until they have committed time |
| Answers the question they actually asked | Answers the question that is convenient |
| Makes one ask, once | Asks three times, in three colours |

**The uncomfortable test:** read your copy and ask *"what did this give the person who did not
buy?"* If the answer is nothing, it is not copy. It is a trap with good manners.

## 2. What the masters actually teach (and what they don't)

These seven are not a canon to worship. They are seven different answers to one question:
*how do you get a stranger to believe something and act on it?* Each is marked with what is
**transferable** and what is **not**, because most of their work was for print and mail, and the
medium changes what works.

### Gary Halbert — the market comes before the words
**Confidence: High** (his own letters; *The Boron Letters* are letters to his son Bond written from
a prison camp and are freely available).

His most-quoted lesson: if he could have any advantage in a new venture he would choose **a
starving crowd** over a great product. The market, not the copy, decides. And: write as one person
talking to one person.

- **Take:** before a word is written, answer *who is starving for this, and what are they already
  trying to buy?* For you the starving crowd is not "small businesses". It is **a vendor who lost
  three customers this week and does not know why.**
- **Leave:** his long-form sales letters and his carnival energy. A 900-word letter is a mailing
  piece, not a caption.

### David Ogilvy — research, and one plain fact beats ten adjectives
**Confidence: High** (*Ogilvy on Advertising*, *Confessions of an Advertising Man*).

He is the antidote to everything else in this file being cute. Two of his lines matter more than
the rest:

> **"The consumer isn't a moron; she is your wife."**

> **"When you advertise fire-extinguishers, open with the fire."**

And his Rolls-Royce headline — *"At 60 miles an hour the loudest noise in this new Rolls-Royce comes
from the electric clock"* — is a fact, not a flourish. It works because it is checkable and odd.

- **Take:** research until you have a **specific true detail**, then lead with it. Never write about
  a category when you can write about one instance.
- **Leave:** his budget, his photographers, his 1950s formality.

### Eugene Schwartz — desire and awareness
**Confidence: High** (*Breakthrough Advertising*, 1966).

Two ideas, and the second is the most useful thing in this whole file:

1. **Copy cannot create desire.** It can only channel desire that already exists toward a specific
   thing.
2. **There are five stages of awareness**, and the same offer needs completely different copy at
   each one: unaware → problem-aware → solution-aware → product-aware → most aware.

He also described **market sophistication**: the first person to make a claim gets it; the fifth
person saying the same thing has to say something new or say it better.

- **Take:** decide the awareness stage *before* writing. A cold DM goes to someone **problem-aware**
  at best. A person who has watched you for a week is **product-aware**. They cannot get the same
  script.
- **Leave:** his long-page examples and his 1960s mail-order offers.

### Claude Hopkins — proof, and being first to say the true thing
**Confidence: High** (*Scientific Advertising*, 1923 — public domain).

Hopkins was hired to sell Schlitz beer. The breweries all steam-cleaned their bottles; none said so.
He wrote about the steam-cleaning, in detail, and Schlitz went from fifth to first. **The claim was
true for every competitor. He was simply the first to say it.**

> *"Advertising is salesmanship in print."*

And on style, he was ruthless: fine writing is a disadvantage, because the reader should be thinking
about the product, not admiring the sentence.

- **Take:** look for the true thing everyone in your market does silently and never says. That is
  the cheapest advantage that exists — and it is exactly why this account can work with no clients:
  you can say what everyone else is too polite, or too busy, to name.
- **Leave:** his coupons and his mail-order measurement.

### John Caples — test headlines, do not defend them
**Confidence: High** (*Tested Advertising Methods*, 1932).

His famous headline — *"They Laughed When I Sat Down at the Piano — But When I Started to Play!"* —
worked because it is a story in one line, and because it names a **fear of being laughed at**, which
is stronger than any promise of skill.

- **Take:** write five headlines, not one, and let the audience pick. You already do this
  mechanically (`npm run audit:november`); Caples is the reason to keep doing it.
- **Leave:** nothing much. His method is the most portable of the seven.

### Joseph Sugarman — the slippery slide
**Confidence: High** (*The Adweek Copywriting Handbook*).

> **"The sole purpose of the first sentence is to get you to read the second sentence."**

He sold BluBlocker sunglasses with long copy that people read to the end, because each line opened a
small loop the next line closed. He called it the slippery slide: once you start, it is easier to
keep reading than to stop.

- **Take:** judge every line by one question — *does it make the next line inevitable?* If a
  sentence could be deleted without anyone noticing, delete it.
- **Leave:** the length. On a phone, the slide is three sentences long, and then it is a swipe.

### Robert Cialdini — the mechanics of trust, and where they turn into manipulation
**Confidence: High** (*Influence*, 1984; the later edition adds **unity** — shared identity).

The principles: reciprocity, commitment and consistency, social proof, authority, liking, scarcity,
and unity.

**But the honest half matters more than the list.** Cialdini's own framing is that these are
*shortcuts people use to decide*, and that using them to deceive is manipulation. Concretely, for
this account:

| Principle | Honest use | Manipulation |
|---|---|---|
| **Reciprocity** | Publish the method free, daily, forever | "Free" report that is a sales letter |
| **Social proof** | Show real comments, real DMs, with permission | Invented testimonials, borrowed logos |
| **Authority** | Show your working, in public, so it can be checked | Claim credentials you do not have |
| **Scarcity** | State a true limit ("I take three a week") | "Only 2 spots left" when there are 20 |
| **Unity** | "Lagos vendors" — a real shared identity you belong to | Pretending to be part of a group you are not |

- **Take:** reciprocity and unity are your two levers this month, because they are the only two that
  work with **zero customers**. You cannot borrow authority you have not earned, and you must not
  invent social proof.
- **Leave:** scarcity games. They spend the one asset you have, which is being believed.

This is the table you asked for, with the Easywurld instruction attached to each. "Instruction"
means: this is what the system must do, not what it must know about.

| # | Module | What it must produce | The Easywurld instruction |

| 1 | **Market research** | Desires, frustrations, objections, alternatives, buying behaviour | Do not guess. Use `../positioning/PAIN-POINTS.md` verbatim quotes, or the 40-minute interview. If neither exists, say so and write a post that asks. |
| 2 | **Customer psychology** | Fears, identity, perceived risk | The number one fear in this market is **being scammed**. Every piece of copy must survive "is this person real?" |
| 3 | **Headlines and hooks** | Curiosity, specificity, contrast | ≤12 words. One mechanism per post. No greeting, no vague drama. See `04-HOOKS.md`. |
| 4 | **Persuasive structure** | PAS, AIDA, before–after–bridge, belief reversal | Default to **Problem → Cost → Fix** for teach, and **Turn** (kishōtenketsu) for entertain. Never stack two structures in 30 seconds. |
| 5 | **Benefits and offers** | Feature → benefit → outcome; risk reduction | Convert every feature into what the reader stops losing. "Reply template" → "you stop losing the customer who asked at 11pm." |
| 6 | **Proof and credibility** | Evidence, demonstration, specificity | With no clients, proof = **the work itself, in public**, + the free sample + a face + a real number. Never a borrowed logo. |
| 7 | **Objection handling** | Price, trust, timing, suitability, effort | Answer the objection *before* it is raised, in one line, without defensiveness. "It is ₦5,000 and it will not bring you new customers" closes more than urgency does. |
| 8 | **Storytelling** | Conflict, stakes, transformation, payoff | Use `03-STORYTELLING.md`. One turn per 30 seconds. The Witness is the strongest and least used. |
| 9 | **Conversion** | Clear CTA, buying instructions, follow-up | **One offer, one price, one word to send.** Say exactly what happens after they send it, and how fast. |
| 10 | **Editing** | Clarity, rhythm, filler removal | Read it aloud. Cut every adverb. Cut every sentence that begins "I just wanted to". If a sentence survives deletion, it was doing nothing. |
| 11 | **Ethical persuasion** | No fake scarcity, no invented proof | Enforced by `npm run copy:check` and `npm run story`. This is not a preference; it is a build gate. |
| 12 | **Testing** | Compare hooks and messages against real responses | One variable at a time. Log it. The tracker is the only honest judge in this repo. |

## 4. The standard: five rewrites

Read the left column. It is not badly written — it is **empty**. That is the difference that matters.

### 1 — The offer itself

| Generic | Easywurld |
|---|---|
| "Get our Marketing Leak Finder today. It will help improve your marketing." | "Someone asked the price four days ago and you never answered. That is the leak. For ₦5,000 I read your page and your last twenty chats, and send back the three fixes and the exact words to copy. Two days." |

**What changed:** a specific scene, a named cost, a price, a deliverable, a timeframe. Nothing else.

### 2 — The caption

| Generic | Easywurld |
|---|---|
| "Consistency is key when it comes to growing your business online. Keep posting!" | "Posting daily will not fix an unanswered price. Forty people asked. None got a number." |

### 3 — The DM

| Generic | Easywurld |
|---|---|
| "Hi, I'm a digital marketer and I help businesses grow. Are you interested?" | "Good afternoon. Your Google listing says closed but your page says you are open. That is costing you walk-ins. Want the three other things I would change? No charge." |

**What changed:** it starts with something about *them* that they can verify in ten seconds.

### 4 — The bio

| Generic | Easywurld |
|---|---|
| "Digital marketing agency | SEO | Social media | Web design | Branding | 💯" | "I read your page and tell you the truth. ₦5,000 to fix it." |

### 5 — The limit

| Generic | Easywurld |
|---|---|
| "100% satisfaction guaranteed!" | "It will not bring you new customers. It stops you losing the ones already messaging you." |

That last one is the most valuable rewrite in this file. **A stated limit outperforms a promise**,
because in a market where everyone promises, the person who names the limit is the only one who
sounds like they have been there.

## 5. The mechanics, as rules you can apply in seconds

1. **Write to one person, out loud.** If you would not say the sentence to a specific vendor in
   Yaba, do not write it.
2. **Specific beats superlative, always.** Not "affordable" — "₦5,000". Not "fast" — "two days". Not
   "many customers" — "forty people asked".
3. **The reader should outnumber you.** Count "you/your" against "I/we/our". If you win, rewrite.
   *(This is checked automatically for every sell post.)*
4. **One ask per post.** One offer, one price, one word to send.
5. **The first line makes a promise the last line keeps.**
6. **State the limit next to the price.** It is not a disclaimer, it is the sales argument.
7. **No sentence that could be deleted.** Sugarman's test.
8. **Say what happens next.** "Send SILENCE and I reply within the hour with three questions." The
   unknown is the biggest drop-off point in any funnel.
9. **Read it aloud.** If you stumble, the reader has already left.
10. **Never claim what you cannot show.** `npm run copy:check` fails the build on hype.

## 6. The editing pass (five minutes, every post)

| Pass | Ask | Delete |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Which word can a reader picture?** | Every abstraction: "solutions", "value", "growth" |
| 2 | **Which sentence is the advertiser talking about himself?** | All of it, or move it to the end |
| 3 | **Which line is a hedge?** | "kind of", "very", "really", "actually", "just", "I think" |
| 4 | **Which sentence repeats the one before it?** | The weaker one |
| 5 | **Read aloud, at speed** | Anything you stumble on |

Then one more: **cut the first sentence and see if it is better.** Half the time it is — because the
real first line is usually the second one.

## 7. What we will not do, even when it works

| Tactic | Why not, in one line |
|---|---|
| Fake scarcity ("only 2 slots!") | You have one asset: being believed. |
| Invented testimonials | One caught lie makes every real number suspect. |
| Borrowed logos | Not yours, not proof, and illegal. |
| "Guaranteed results" | You cannot guarantee someone else's business. |
| Clickbait that withholds the answer | Costs you the next video, and the algorithm now measures early exit. |
| Manufactured urgency on a ₦5,000 product | If it is urgent, say why. If it is not, do not pretend. |
| Making the customer the villain | They are the hero. The villain is the silence. |

## 8. How this changes what we already built

**Nothing in the 330 posts has to be rewritten.** They were built with most of this already:
limits stated, prices shown, one idea per post, reader-first in the sell tier.

What this file changes is **everything written from now on**, and three things immediately:

1. **The offer.** Eighteen products is not a decision, it is a menu. One offer — see
   [`07-THE-ONE-OFFER.md`](./07-THE-ONE-OFFER.md).
2. **The DM.** The three messages in `../outreach/MESSAGES.txt` now end with one word, not a choice.
3. **The check.** `npm run copy:check` runs over the whole month and fails on hype, on vague words,
   on a sell post that talks about us instead of the reader, and on more than one offer in the month.

---

## 9. The one-sentence standard

> **Name a specific cost the reader can verify, put one price next to one limit, and ask once.**

If a piece of copy does not do that, it is not finished. It is typed.
