## OBS-001 — The cardboard video

**Date logged:** 28 October 2026 · **Source:** you, on TikTok · **Verification:** none. I cannot open
TikTok and did not see it. **Status: reported.**

> A child holding a bitten piece of cardboard; the video made it look as though she was eating it.
> You waited 20–30 seconds. Then she pulled out what she was actually eating, and the video ended.

**Your read, in your words:** *"For it to go viral, it should be something that is unexpected but
still expected… If we had to use an adult, people would expect that, okay, he's eating something
else. But if you use someone dressed like a madman or a child, people expect that they will eat
anything."*

**The mechanic I extracted:** three parts — a formed pattern, a break that the *character* makes
believable, and a held question that is answered before the end. Written up in
[`01-PATTERN-INTERRUPTION.md`](./01-PATTERN-INTERRUPTION.md).

**Why your read is the valuable part:** it is the difference between a surprise and a stunt. Most
people copy the weird image and not the casting, and then it reads as fake.

**Unresolved:** I do not know if the account was new, how long the setup ran before the reveal, or
whether it was a single take. If you find it again, note the seconds.

## OBS-002 — The villain and the hero

**Date logged:** 28 October 2026 · **Source:** you, a woman's video (not named) ·
**Verification:** none. **Status: reported.**

> Create a common enemy, and a hero to fight that enemy. Get people on the side of either the hero
> or the villain — but we stay in the middle.

**Your hesitation, in your words:** *"It should be in a way that if we don't join hands, we cannot
actually solve the problem. But I don't want it to feel like you're now depending on them."*

**The mechanic I extracted:** three roles, not two — villain, hero, guide. The villain is the
system, the hero is the customer, and we are the guide. That is what removes the neediness, because
nobody is asking anybody for anything. Written up in [`02-VILLAIN-AND-HERO.md`](./02-VILLAIN-AND-HERO.md).

**Note on the source:** the advice is a familiar framework, closest to Donald Miller's *Building a
StoryBrand* — "the customer is the hero, your brand is the guide" — and to the older practice of
naming an industry's shared enemy. Nothing about it is secret. What she added, and what you caught,
is the "we stay in the middle" part.

## OBS-005 — "Just one offer"

**Date logged:** 28 October 2026 · **Source:** you · **Status:** adopted, migration incomplete

> "I also want to give just one offer we can use — don't confuse them with more than one option to
> choose from. And also to care for them."

**What I made of it:** a choice is work, and the cheapest thing a stranger can do is decide later —
which means never. Five offers do not get five times the attention; they get a fifth each.

**The offer chosen:** **The Silence Fix, ₦5,000** — you send your page and your last twenty chats, I
send back three fixes in order plus the exact words to copy. Two days. One word to send: `SILENCE`.
Written up in [`07-THE-ONE-OFFER.md`](./07-THE-ONE-OFFER.md).

**Why this one and not a pack:** it needs no file to exist, so it cannot be blocked by the missing
`packkit` repo — and it is the only offer that attacks the villain directly.

**Measured state:** `npm run copy:check` reports the month currently sells **eight** things across
30 sell posts. The conversion is a specific job, not a vibe: 30 posts, one word each, and the checker
goes green when it is done.

## R-001 — Curiosity is a specific gap, not a mood

**Source:** George Loewenstein, "The Psychology of Curiosity", *Psychological Bulletin*, 1994 ·
**Confidence: High** · **Used in:** `01`, `04`

Curiosity fires when you become aware of a **specific** gap between what you know and what you want
to know. It feels uncomfortable, like an itch, not like interest. Three conditions: you must know
enough to sense the gap, the gap must be specific, and it must feel closeable.

**Why it matters here:** "something amazing happens" creates no gap, which is why vague hooks are
not just weak — they are inert.

## R-002 — The orienting response

**Source:** established psychology (Sokolov and later work); commercialised as "pattern interrupt" in
sales training · **Confidence: High** on the reflex, **Low** on every specific retention number
quoted by agencies · **Used in:** `01`

Novel or unexpected stimuli pull attention automatically, before conscious thought. Familiar stimuli
stop being noticed (habituation).

**Why it matters here:** you cannot be too boring to be noticed and too boring to be ignored at the
same time. Boredom is exactly what the feed stops showing.

## R-003 — The customer is the hero, the brand is the guide

**Source:** Donald Miller, *Building a StoryBrand* (2017) · **Confidence: Medium–High** as a
framework; it is not a measured result · **Used in:** `02`

The villain must be a **root cause**: real, singular, relatable, and never the customer.

**Why it matters here:** it answers your question about neediness. A guide does not need the hero.
A guide hands over a tool and leaves.

## R-004 — Kishōtenketsu

**Source:** classical East Asian four-part narrative form (起承転結), well documented ·
**Confidence: High** · **Used in:** `03`, and one worked script

Introduction → development → twist → reconciliation. It does **not require conflict**. The engine is
re-framing, not fighting.

**Why it matters here:** it is the shape of your cardboard video, and it is the shape your 210
entertain posts should take. No villain, no lesson, just a turn.

## R-007 — The seven copywriting masters

**Source:** primary books and letters, not summaries — Halbert (*The Boron Letters*), Ogilvy
(*Ogilvy on Advertising*), Schwartz (*Breakthrough Advertising*, 1966), Hopkins (*Scientific
Advertising*, 1923), Caples (*Tested Advertising Methods*, 1932), Sugarman (*The Adweek Copywriting
Handbook*), Cialdini (*Influence*, 1984) · **Confidence: High** on what they wrote,
**Medium** on how much any single technique explains modern social performance · **Used in:** `06`

**The one idea that reorganises everything for this account:** Schwartz's five stages of awareness.
A cold DM reaches someone **problem-aware at best**. A person who has watched you for a week is
**product-aware**. They cannot receive the same script — and the current DM script assumes the
second when it is talking to the first.

**Caveat that must stay attached:** most of these men wrote for print and mail, to audiences with no
feed competing for attention. The *principles* transfer; the *formats* do not.

## R-008 — Where persuasion becomes manipulation

**Source:** Cialdini, *Influence* (1984; unity added in the 2016 edition) · **Confidence: High** ·
**Used in:** `06` §2, `08`

The seven principles are decision shortcuts, not tricks. Used to deceive, they are manipulation.
**The line this account draws:** reciprocity and unity only, because they are the only two that work
with zero customers — you cannot borrow authority you have not earned, and social proof you have not
got is a lie.
