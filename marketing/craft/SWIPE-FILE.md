# The swipe file

*Copy that works, annotated with **why** it works — not so we can copy it, so we can steal the
mechanism.*

**The honesty rule for this file:** only real ads with real attribution go in. No invented examples,
no "a client of mine", no reconstructed quotes. If I am not sure of the exact wording, it says so.
Where a line is paraphrased, it is marked and it still says why it works.

**How to use it:** when a post is not landing, find the mechanism in the right-hand column and apply
*that* to your topic. Never lift the sentence — your reader is not a 1950s housewife, and a borrowed
line reads as a costume.

---

## 1. Specificity

### Rolls-Royce — David Ogilvy, 1958
> "At 60 miles an hour the loudest noise in this new Rolls-Royce comes from the electric clock."

**Mechanism:** one verifiable fact, stated flatly, with no adjective. "Luxury" is a claim; "the clock
is louder than the engine at 60mph" is evidence.

**Your version:** "This shop answers every price question in nine minutes. The one next to it takes
three days. Same product, same street."

### Schlitz — Claude Hopkins, c. 1907
Described the brewery's **steam-cleaned bottles**, in detail. Every competitor did it; none said so.

**Mechanism:** being *first to say the true thing everyone does silently*. The claim does not need to
be unique. The saying does.

**Your version:** "Every vendor I have looked at this week replies 'DM for price'. Not one of them
has ever said why."

---

## 2. The story in one line

### U.S. School of Music — John Caples, 1926
> "They Laughed When I Sat Down at the Piano — But When I Started to Play!"

**Mechanism:** a whole story in a headline, with the **fear of embarrassment** as the engine — not
the promise of skill. Note the "but": Caples built the entire ad on the same connector this account
runs on.

**Your version:** "She posted the price and her cousin laughed. Then the orders came." *(Only if it
happened. Otherwise write the true version.)*

### "Do You Make These Mistakes In English?" — Sherwin Cody, 1919
**Mechanism:** a question that is impossible to answer "no" to, and which makes the reader check
themselves. One of the most-run ads in history.

**Your version:** "Does your page say what you sell in four seconds? Check with a stranger. Then
tell me the answer." *(Risky: it can feel like a trick. Use at most once a month.)*

---

## 3. The slippery slide

### BluBlocker — Joseph Sugarman, 1980s
Long copy, read to the end, because every line opened a loop the next one closed.

**Mechanism:** each sentence exists to get the next one read. Sugarman's own words: the sole purpose
of the first sentence is to get you to read the second sentence.

**Your version, in three sentences:** "A customer asked for the price. Nobody answered for four
days. Here is the message that would have kept them."

---

## 4. Naming the enemy

### Wise (formerly TransferWise), 2010s
Sold against **hidden bank fees** — not against a competitor. The villain was a *practice*, described
plainly enough that people recognised something they had felt but never named.

**Mechanism:** the villain is a condition, not a company. Naming it earns the comment "this is me".

**Your version:** the silence after "how much?" — see `02-VILLAIN-AND-HERO.md`.

### Allstate — "Mayhem", 2005 onward
Personified the cause of accidents as a character, so the brand could be the guide without ever
insulting the customer.

**Mechanism:** personify the villain, never the customer. (A character is optional for you — the
*format* memes do the same job: see `../november/WHAT-WE-TEACH.md`.)

---

## 5. The admitted limit

### Avis — "We Try Harder", 1962 (Doyle Dane Bernbach)
> "Avis is only No.2 in rent a car. So we try harder."

**Mechanism:** lead with the weakness, and convert it into the reason to buy. It works only if the
weakness is *true* and the reason is *specific*. Every "honest ad" post in this account is this
mechanism.

**Your version:** "I have no client results to show you. So judge the method instead — it is more
checkable than a logo."

### Volkswagen Beetle — "Lemon", 1960
Ran a photo of a car with a defect and explained the inspection that caught it.

**Mechanism:** advertising the rejection standard. Showing what you *refuse* is stronger proof than
showing what you accept.

**Your version:** "I turned down ₦180,000. Here is what they wanted and why I said no." *(This is
D1-10 in your build — same mechanism, 65 years later.)*

---

## 6. The reader-first opener

### "You" — the oldest rule in direct mail
Halbert, Hopkins, Caples and Ogilvy all arrive at the same instruction from different directions: write
to one person, in the second person, about their life and not yours.

**Mechanism:** the reader should outnumber the writer in pronouns. This is checked automatically in
`npm run copy:check` for every sell post.

**Your version:** every bio, caption and DM in this repo now begins with something about them.

## 7. What is NOT in this file, and why

| Left out | Why |
|---|---|
| "Only 3 left!" scarcity ads | The mechanism works and it spends the only asset this account has |
| Weight-loss / get-rich before-and-afters | Unverifiable, and a ₦5,000 product does not need them |
| "Doctors hate this one trick" | Curiosity with no payoff. Costs the next video. |
| Any guru's personal funnel copy | It is engineered for selling courses about funnels. Different business. |
| Anything I could not attribute | This file is only useful if it is trustworthy |

## 8. Adding to it

When you see an ad, a post, a DM or a headline that stops **you**:

1. Copy the **exact words**, not a summary.
2. Say where you saw it and when.
3. Answer in one line: *what is the mechanism?* — specificity, story, enemy, limit, proof, loop.
4. Say what it would look like for a Lagos vendor.

Send it to me and I will add it here with the analysis. Three or four a month is plenty; this file is
worth more as twelve annotated examples than as a hundred screenshots nobody read.
