# The three accounts — what they do, what to steal, what to ignore

Research note · 24 October 2026

**What I could and could not see.** TikTok returns `403` to every request I can make, so I could
not open the profiles or watch the videos directly. Everything below comes from indexed page
titles, captions, video titles and interviews. Two of the three resolved clearly. **The two
`vt.tiktok.com` links you sent did not resolve at all** — tell me what they were and I will
fold them in. Nothing here is a guess dressed up as a fact; where I am inferring, I say so.

## 1. @personalbrandlaunch0 — "Personal Brand Launch" (Ava)

**Who:** a social media management agency, `pblaunch.com`. ~283K TikTok followers, ~2.6M likes,
and roughly 849K on Instagram under `@personalbrandlaunch`. (Follower counts as reported in
indexed sources, so treat them as approximate.)

**What the content actually is** — every title is a mechanic of short-form, taught out loud:

> "Written Hooks 101" · "Steal These Viral Hooks" · "100 View v.s. 100K View v.s. 1M View Hook"
> · "Same Hook, Different Delivery" · "What Format Goes Most Viral?" · "Sound Effects 101" ·
> "Editing Effects 101" · "How Big Creators Go Viral On Repeat" · "Personal Branding Bible,
> Episode #1"

**The business model:** they sell **social media management** — "we will research, script, edit
and upload for you". The call to action is a comment keyword ("Comment SMM"). Their best-
performing posts are the ones that reveal a mechanism the viewer can use in the next ten minutes.

**What to steal**

1. **Series, with numbers.** "Personal Branding Bible — Episode #1" turns one good idea into a
   season. A season is a reason to follow, not just to watch.
2. **The reveal format.** Handing over a real mechanic ("here are the three hooks, side by side,
   at 100 views / 100K / 1M") is the single most saveable format in this niche. Saves are a
   distribution signal, and saved posts get re-read.
3. **The comment-keyword CTA.** It converts a passive viewer into a private conversation, which
   is the only thing that sells a service. You already have WhatsApp; a comment keyword is the
   same idea with a public on-ramp.

**What to ignore**

- Their audience is **creators and agencies**, not small-business owners. Easywurld's buyer runs
  a food business or a salon and does not care what "format goes most viral". Take the *structure*,
  change the *subject*.
- "Steal these hooks" works for them because their whole product is content. For you it risks
  looking like a content account rather than a studio that builds things.

## 2. @natecrissey7 — Nate Crissey

**Who:** a videographer who runs a social media marketing agency, posting with `#tampa`. Smaller
than the other two (one indexed video at ~21K views), so treat the format as proven more than the
scale.

**What the content actually is:**

> "What I would do if I started over in videography today" · "The top services a social media
> marketing agency should offer: management, content systems, event coverage, ad creation,
> editing/clipping" · "No other marketing form gives you that much reach and return"

**What to steal — and this is the most useful of the three for you**

1. **"What I would do if I started over"** is the **story spine in disguise**. It is a
   first-person causal chain ("I did X, but Y, therefore Z") with no client, no case study and no
   permission needed. It is the cheapest honest authority content that exists.
2. **Naming your own service menu on camera.** He lists what an agency should offer. That is a
   pricing conversation disguised as education — it pre-sells the exact packages you are trying
   to launch, without quoting a price.
3. **Local identity.** `#tampa` is doing work. Lagos is a stronger badge than Tampa, and you are
   not using it as heavily as you could.

## 3. @adley — Adley Kinsman (Viralish)

**Who:** founder of Viralish, Nashville. Reports **1B+ organic views a month since 2020**, 75+
videos over 100M views, and a claimed seven-in-ten hit rate using her framework. She is the most
rigorous of the three and the only one whose method is written down.

**Her six-step "Billion View Formula"** (from the Social Media Examiner interview and related
podcast appearances):

| # | Step | The claim |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook for 90% retention in 6 seconds** | Nothing else matters if this fails |
| 2 | **Suspenseful storytelling** (the "Missy Elliott method" — put it down, flip it, reverse it) | Start mid-action; never open with the result |
| 3 | **The payoff**, in the final 3–10 seconds | Where the promise is kept |
| 4 | **Engineer engagement** | Design the comment section in reverse |
| 5 | **Raise the stakes** | Location, props, casting, wardrobe — why anyone cares |
| 6 | **Test before you post** | Multiple hooks, compare retention |

**The two mechanics worth engraving:**

> *"Your first three seconds have to create an itch that you do not scratch until the last three
> seconds."*

> **The combo method:** every hook needs two things that do not normally go together — a curiosity
> gap that will not be satisfied until the end, and an emotional response. Her example: a rack of
> ribs on a car engine.

**And the philosophical position, which is your thesis, already stated by someone with a billion
views a month:**

> "If you want your videos to reach everyone, make what people want to watch."

**What to steal**

- The **itch/scratch rule** — this is the spine of [`../storytelling/THE-ITCH.md`](../storytelling/THE-ITCH.md).
- **60%+ top-of-funnel.** Her explicit split. Yours is in the same document.
- **Start mid-action.** Never "today I want to talk about". Always "the second line of your
  homepage is costing you".
- **Four hooks per idea.** She writes and shoots multiple openings and compares retention. You
  cannot run her focus groups, but you can write three hooks for every post and pick one.

**What to ignore**

- **Her economics.** She built a machine on Facebook's three-minute monetisation window, which
  paid per watch-minute. That is not your business. Do not make three-minute videos because she
  did — your buyer is on WhatsApp, not in a video ad programme.
- **Production stakes.** Props, casting, wardrobe and locations cost money she has. Your version of
  raising the stakes is being *in an actual Lagos market with an actual trader*, which costs you a
  bus fare.
- **The "10 psychological hook points".** It is referenced in interviews but the list is not
  published in the sources I could read. I have not invented it, and neither should you.

## 4. The pattern all three share (the actual finding)

Read the three side by side and the interesting thing is not the hooks. It is this:

> **All three are in the same business as Easywurld — social, web and marketing services — and
> none of them makes its money selling a ₦5,000 download.**

They sell **management retainers**, **agency services** and **production**. The educational content
is the top of a funnel that ends in a service contract. The products, when they exist, are
secondary.

That is the second piece of evidence for the plan in [`../EASYWURLD-PLAN.md`](../EASYWURLD-PLAN.md):
the offers are the front door, the services are the room you want people to walk into. And it is
the reason the entertainment tier matters more than it feels like it should — entertainment is the
only tier that reaches people who do not yet know they need a website.

**A second shared pattern, more tactical:** all three post in a **small number of repeating
formats**, endlessly. One format, many subjects. That is what makes batching possible and what
makes an account recognisable after three seconds of scrolling.

## 5. What transfers to Lagos, and what does not

| Transfers directly | Does not transfer |
|---|---|
| The itch/scratch retention structure | US production budgets and prop libraries |
| But/therefore causation | Facebook 3-minute monetisation economics |
| 60% top-of-funnel ratio | "Creator economy" income assumptions |
| Repeating formats over novelty | Focus-group testing at four hooks per video |
| Comment-keyword → DM → service sale | Their audiences: US creators, not Lagos SME owners |
| "What I would do if I started over" | The cultural references and idioms |

**The one thing that is yours and none of theirs:** you are in Lagos, selling to Lagos. Their
content is generic because it has to travel. Yours does not have to. A video filmed in Balogun
market, with a trader's actual words in it, beats any of the three on relevance — for your buyer.
