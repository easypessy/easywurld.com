# Why this works — and what would make it fail

**The strategy, tested against research.**
24 October 2026 · the gate document before the 30-day build

---

## 1. Your sentence, and my verdict

You said:

> **We entertain everyone, because they're not on social to be sold to. Then we teach the interested, and sell the committed.**

**Verdict: yes. This is right, and it is the correct correction to everything that came before.**

It is right for three reasons you can check:

1. It matches how people actually behave on these platforms (evidence in §2).
2. It matches the oldest useful model of buying in direct response — Schwartz's five stages of awareness (evidence in §3).
3. It solves the specific problem Easywurld has: nobody knows you yet. Every other tier assumes an audience you don't have.

**But it needs one word changed, and three conditions attached.** That is what the rest of this page is for.

**The word:** not "entertain everyone." → **"Entertain everyone *you can sell to*."**

Entertainment that isn't about your buyer's world builds an audience that will never buy. A million people laughing is not a business. The discipline is: **narrow in subject, wide in appeal.** The joke, the story, the teardown is always about a business trying to sell something — but it is made funny or tense enough that a stranger who has never run a business still watches to the end.

That single constraint is the difference between Easywurld and a comedy account.

## 2. "People aren't on social to be sold to" — is that true?

**Yes.** Two independent sources:

**TikTok's own platform doctrine.** Their entire brand message to advertisers since 2019 is *"Don't make ads. Make TikToks."* Their own explanation: the mantra was "an invitation to be more creative, more authentic, and to create content that truly speaks to people." A creator quoted in their own post says the quiet part: *"TikTok is a totally different audience that expects authenticity and doesn't like pushy advertising."*

**The research on intent.** A Saatchi report surveying 1,000 US TikTok users put it best:

> "A lot of people go to TikTok for content. They're there for entertainment, to learn and watch content that supports whatever they're feeling at the moment. **They're not there specifically to shop**, but it's a clever way to introduce your products organically and drive purchase intent."

Note the second half of that sentence, because it is the part people skip: *"it's a clever way to introduce your products organically and drive purchase intent."* The same report found 56% of users had bought something based on a TikTok video, and 92% had taken some action.

**So the honest reading is not "you can't sell on social". It is: you cannot *open* by selling, and you cannot *only* sell. The platform punishes the interruption and rewards the introduction.**

That is exactly what your three-tier sentence says.

## 3. "Teach the interested, sell the committed" — is that a real model?

**Yes. It is Eugene Schwartz's five stages of awareness, from *Breakthrough Advertising* (1966), and it has survived 60 years because it is mechanically true.**

HIs rule is the one that matters to you: **the message must start where the buyer already is**. A message written for one stage reads as noise to a prospect in any other.

| Schwartz stage | What they know | Your tier | What the post must do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Unaware** | Nothing. No felt problem | **Entertain** | Start with *them* — a story, a scene, a symptom. Never the product |
| **Problem aware** | Feels the pain, doesn't know a fix exists | **Entertain → Teach** | Name the pain better than they can |
| **Solution aware** | Knows fixes exist, not yours | **Teach** | Show the mechanism. Why this way works |
| **Product aware** | Knows your packs, isn't sure | **Sell (soft)** | Remove the objection. State the limit |
| **Most aware** | Wants it, needs the last nudge | **Sell (direct)** | The price, the word, the link. Nothing else |

Your sentence maps onto this exactly — *entertain everyone* (unaware + problem aware) → *teach the interested* (solution aware) → *sell the committed* (product + most aware). That is not a coincidence. You arrived by instinct at a model that direct-response has used for six decades.

**One correction to the common version of this table.** Many articles claim the market splits 60/20/10/7/3 across those stages. **That distribution is folklore** — it is not in *Breakthrough Advertising*, and no primary source exists for it. Do not use those percentages to plan. What *is* defensible is directional: prospecting channels skew early-stage, and search/retargeting skew late-stage.

So your 60/30/10 split is a **starting ratio to test**, not a law. Which is exactly why we built the scorecard to move it.

*Sources:* [Schwartz applied](https://growthmarketer.co/stages-of-awareness/) · [stage-by-stage table](https://www.optimum7.com/blog/5-levels-of-customer-awareness-and-how-to-target-them.html) · [the folklore warning](https://www.getlandra.com/blog/5-stages-of-awareness) · [format fit by stage](https://www.exposure.com.au/resources/the-5-awareness-stages)

## 5. The Nigeria data — and the thing it changes about my last plan

This is the part I got wrong before, and it is more important than any of the above.

| Platform | Adult ad reach in Nigeria | Direction | Share of Nigerians using it (Pew 2026) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **WhatsApp** | ~98% of internet users | flat, dominant | — |
| **TikTok** | **47.8M** | **+43.4% in a year** | 57% (was 25% in 2023) |
| **Facebook** | **38.0M** | +6.9% | **72%** |
| YouTube | 30.5M | +13.0% | — |
| **Instagram** | **10.0M** | **−6.1%** | 40% |
| LinkedIn | — | — | 31.3% self-declared use |

**What this changes:**

1. **Instagram is not your main platform. It is a third of TikTok's reach and shrinking.** My November plan gave it equal weight. That was wrong for Lagos in 2026.
2. **TikTok is the reach engine** — nearly five times Instagram's adult reach, and growing 43% a year. Entertain-first belongs here first.
3. **Facebook is the conversion platform.** Highest usage share of any platform in Nigeria (72%), and it is where older, established business owners — the ones who can pay ₦150,000 — actually are.
4. **Dropping LinkedIn is correct.** 31.3% self-declared use, and it is not where a Lagos salon or food vendor decides anything. You lose almost nothing.
5. **WhatsApp is not a fourth option, it is the checkout.** ~98% of Nigerian internet users. Your status is a distribution channel and your inbox is the till.

**Revised weighting for the month:** TikTok carries the entertainment (reach), Facebook carries the teaching and selling (conversion), Instagram gets the best of both (smallest reach, highest-intent audience in Lagos), WhatsApp status carries whatever's working.

## 6. The five ways this fails (read this twice)

A strategy is only worth following if you know how it dies.

**1. You entertain the wrong people.**
The most likely failure. A funny video about generic office life reaches students who will never buy. **Fix:** every entertainment post names a business situation in the first four seconds. If a 22-year-old who has never sold anything can't still enjoy it — good, it's still the right post; if you've removed the business entirely, it's the wrong post.

**2. You are not actually entertaining.**
"Entertain" is not a tier you get for free by adding a joke to a lesson. It is the hardest tier — best hooks, best edits, best ideas — and it is where most accounts quietly die. A mediocre teaching post still teaches. A mediocre entertainment post reaches nobody, and it takes the other two tiers down with it. **Fix:** the quality gate in THE-ITCH §5, and `npm run story` before posting.

**3. The handoff breaks.**
Entertain → teach → sell only works if a viewer can *move between tiers*. On TikTok that bridge is your profile and your pinned posts, not the video. **Fix:** the top pinned post on every profile must be a teach post, and your bio must say what you sell in six words. If someone laughs and then can't find out what you do in three seconds, the entertainment was wasted.

**4. You need money too soon.**
This is the honest cost. Entertainment produces **followers**, not revenue — typically a 4-to-8-week lag before teaching converts them. If you need ₦300,000 this month, entertain-first is the wrong strategy and you should be doing outreach and the free review instead. If you are building for six months, it is the right one. **You cannot run this strategy and be impatient at the same time.**

**5. You mistake reach for traction.**
40,000 views and 2 messages feels like failure when you're posting 11 times a day. It isn't — it's the normal shape at week three. **Fix:** judge entertainment on reach and follows, teaching on conversations, selling on money. Never one number for all three. (The scorecard now enforces this — see `npm run score`.)

## 7. The one thing I would add to your sentence

You said: *"sell the committed."*

**I would change that verb.** At the committed stage you are not selling — they have already decided. You are **removing steps**. Every extra tap between "I want this" and "I have it" loses people who were already yours.

So the final version:

> **We entertain everyone we can sell to, because nobody is on social to be sold to.
> Then we teach whoever leans in.
> Then we make it as easy as possible for the ones who have already decided.**

That third line changes what you build: a payment link, not a better pitch. A one-word keyword, not a longer sales page. It is why `client/src/data/payment.ts` matters more than any copy you will write this month.

## 8. What would prove this wrong (check at day 45)

Agree now, so you can't rationalise later:

| Signal | If it happens | Then |
|---|---|---|
| Entertainment gets reach but **follows don't grow** | People watch and leave | The posts aren't about a person they want to follow. Give the account a face and a point of view |
| Entertainment grows follows but **teaching gets no conversations** | Wrong audience | The entertainment is too broad. Narrow the subject |
| Teaching gets conversations but **nobody buys** | Offer or price problem | Stop posting more. Fix the offer (see the plan, §8) |
| **Everything** works but revenue lags 8 weeks | Normal | Hold. Do not switch strategy at week 4 |

**The single number that settles it:** conversations per 10 posts, split by tier. If the
entertain tier produces zero conversations *and* no follow growth after 30 days of proper
volume, the strategy is failing and we change it — with evidence, not with a feeling.

## 9. So — am I confident?

**Yes, with the conditions in §6 accepted.** Specifically:

- The sequencing is right and is backed by 60 years of direct-response practice plus TikTok's own platform doctrine.
- The ratio is a reasonable start and is measurable, so it can be corrected in six weeks rather than six months.
- The platform weighting has now been corrected with Nigerian data: **TikTok for reach, Facebook for conversion, Instagram third, WhatsApp as the till, LinkedIn dropped.**
- The storytelling structure is written, tested, and enforced by a script.

The one thing I cannot promise is speed. This strategy trades a slow start for a compounding one. If that trade is unacceptable, say so now and we build the outreach-first month instead.
