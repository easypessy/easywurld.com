# 03 — Copywriting & storytelling

**Owner:** Copy / editorial · **Prerequisites:** 00–02 · **Pass evidence:** Message architecture plus channel-ready copy and fact check

## What this department must know

Copywriting turns audience understanding and a truthful offer into useful communication. Strong writing makes a relevant decision clearer; it cannot make an unsuitable offer true. Storytelling organizes information around a change or question; it is not permission to invent a testimonial or imply a real event that never occurred.

### Core skills

- **Message hierarchy:** one audience, one objective, one main idea; support with proof, explanation, qualification and a next step.
- **Offer clarity:** what it is, who it is for, what is included, how it works, price/terms when available, limitations and how to begin.
- **Voice:** intelligent, direct, creative, calm, confident and honest. Prefer active verbs, concrete nouns and natural spoken language; avoid inflated certainty, empty superlatives and agency jargon.
- **Persuasive structures:** problem–insight–solution; before–after–bridge; claim–proof–implication; question–answer–action; story arc. Treat each as an organizing aid, not an evidence-backed guarantee of conversion.
- **Headlines and hooks:** earn attention with relevance, specificity, tension or a useful question. The hook must be paid off; avoid bait-and-switch, outrage bait and unsupported numerical promises.
- **Objections:** answer the actual concern with an accurate answer. Do not conceal a material trade-off or create a straw-man objection.
- **Story craft:** character, desire, obstacle, choice, consequence and change. Use fictional or composite narratives only when clearly identified and appropriate. Keep real customer stories faithful to source and permission.
- **Channel craft:** write for spoken delivery, screen size, reading speed and sound-off viewing. Captions should convey the complete spoken meaning, not just decorative keywords.
- **Editing:** remove duplication, vague claims, passive constructions that obscure responsibility, jargon and unnecessary qualifiers—without removing necessary qualifications.

## Message architecture template

- Audience/context and their language (source-linked).
- Communication task: what should they know, feel, consider or do?
- Main message (one sentence).
- Promise/value and proof (each claim linked to claims register).
- Qualification / exclusions / terms.
- Objection and response.
- CTA, destination, expected effort and fallback.
- Tone, vocabulary to use/avoid, locale and pronunciation notes.
- Channel/length constraints and accessibility needs.

## Claim discipline

For every objective factual or comparative line, record: exact wording; implied meaning; evidence/source; dates and population; owner; required qualifier; expiry/recheck date; and allowed formats. Distinguish experience language (“designed to help”) from outcome claims (“will increase sales”). Do not upgrade the former to a guarantee. Avoid “best,” “first,” “leading,” “proven,” quantified outcomes or competitor comparisons without appropriate current evidence and review.

## Scriptwriting for future video

Write for the ear: one thought per breath, natural contractions where appropriate, pronounceable names, concrete transitions and intentional pauses. A complete script includes target duration, speaker, spoken words, pronunciation, caption transcript and CTA. Time the read aloud; don't assume a fixed words-per-second rate. The director uses the script as one input, not as a reason to render every sentence as a title card.

## Connections

Strategy supplies position and objective; psychology contributes decision insight; copy develops the promise, story and CTA; design makes hierarchy visible; film translates beats into time and action; distribution adapts phrasing to a channel; analytics tests message hypotheses; governance checks the exact words and implied claims.

## Assessment

Create a landing-page hero and a 20–30 second draft script from a supplied approved brief. Provide two different hooks that lead to the same truthful promise; a claims annotation; spoken-time check; accessible caption transcript; and a CTA matched to the destination. **Pass:** audience and offer are clear; proof and qualifier are present; hook pays off; script sounds natural aloud; no invented proof; CTA matches the next step. **Remediate:** fact-check each line and rewrite before creative production.
