# 05 — Cinematography & video craft

**Owner:** Director / production · **Prerequisites:** 00–04 · **Pass evidence:** Complete independently directed video package; no render required for this curriculum

## Non-negotiable production standard

**Do not generate videos as part of this first curriculum assignment.** This module teaches the process only. Every future video is conceived as an independently directed production. Never turn existing carousels into enlarged video slides. A carousel may inform the strategy, but a video needs its own visual idea, performance, action, composition, pacing and sound.

## What this department must know

- **Directing:** translate objective and script into observable action; decide point of view, performance, blocking, frame, pace, tone and audience relationship.
- **Camera language:** shot size, angle, lens perspective, movement, focus, depth, framing, screen direction and continuity. Each choice carries meaning; avoid arbitrary movement.
- **Lighting:** source, direction, softness, contrast, exposure, colour temperature and continuity. Plan practical lighting rather than prompt-only adjectives.
- **Production design:** location, wardrobe, props, texture, background, time and continuity; every object in frame can imply context or evidence.
- **Editing:** selection, action continuity, rhythm, transitions, visual logic, readable hold time, sound edit, colour consistency and delivery versions.
- **Sound:** dialogue intelligibility, room tone, ambience, foley, music role, mix levels, licensing and silence. Music should support the idea without masking speech.
- **Vertical/mobile viewing:** safe zones, subject placement, caption space, first-frame clarity, platform-native interaction and sound-off comprehension.
- **AI video limitations:** motion drift, anatomy errors, inconsistent wardrobe, hands, object permanence, camera mismatch, lip sync, text artifacts and false documentary appearance. Use shot-specific review; regenerate or replace defective footage rather than hiding it with overlays.

## Required production package (all fields)

1. **Production brief:** audience; objective; channel; intended duration; audience state; central idea; CTA/destination; claims and exclusions; required approvals.
2. **Opening hook:** a specific first visual and/or line that earns attention and is fulfilled by the story—not a misleading claim.
3. **Complete voiceover script:** exact words, speaker, delivery, pronunciation, pauses, duration estimate and caption transcript. Script should be naturally speakable and timing-checked.
4. **Scene-by-scene storyboard:** scene number, in/out time, purpose, action, composition, shot size/angle, movement, lens/perspective intention, lighting, environment/wardrobe/props, transition and continuity notes.
5. **Individual generation prompts:** one prompt per shot/clip (not one vague prompt for a whole video). Include subject/action, setting, framing, camera movement, light, palette, duration, aspect ratio, continuity references, do-not-generate constraints, and any model/tool settings. Keep on-screen type out of generated footage unless deliberately tested; add exact text in post.
6. **Timed captions and overlays:** exact text, start/end time, line breaks, safe zone, emphasis and animation; captions must preserve full spoken content and be readable.
7. **Sound plan:** dialogue capture/synthesis rights; ambience and foley; music mood/tempo/role; edit points; mix checks; licenses and attribution.
8. **Edit instructions:** assembly order, pace, cuts/transitions, visual/caption relationship, colour, audio levels, grade, CTA/end card and version list.
9. **Ending:** a clear close and appropriate single CTA; no unresolved claim; allow sufficient time to read and act.
10. **Delivery/review:** frame rate, resolution, codec, aspect ratio, loudness/platform checks as applicable, filename/version, subtitles/caption file, thumbnail/cover, accessibility, rights and named sign-off.

## Storyboard timing template

| Scene / time | Narrative job | Action / performance | Frame & camera | Lighting / art | Voiceover / sound | Captions / overlays | Generation prompt / continuity | Edit / transition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 / 0:00–0:03 | Hook | Specific visible action | Shot, angle, motion | Source, contrast, palette | Exact VO + sound cue | Exact timed copy | Standalone, shot-specific prompt | Cut on action / hold |

Repeat for every scene. The storyboard timecodes must sum to the target duration; do not leave timing implicit.

## Independent visual concept test

Could the film idea be described without referencing slide numbers or enlarging static layouts? Does the camera reveal/change something over time? Is the audience's attention guided by action and composition? If the whole piece is stationary text panels with minor transitions, develop a new film concept.

## Connections

Strategy defines audience response; copy provides spoken beats; design contributes art direction and identity; character lead enforces continuity and anonymity; cinematography plans action and image; audio and captions create access; distribution informs framing/specs; governance verifies claims, rights and disclosures; analytics tests hook/retention/action without treating views as business impact by default.

## Assessment (paper design only)

Prepare a production package for a hypothetical 20-second video without generating or editing video. **Pass rubric:** specific hook, audience and objective; complete natural VO; scene times total 20 seconds; camera/light directions per scene; shot-specific prompts; timed readable captions; sound/music rights notes; explicit independent direction (not an enlarged carousel); coherent ending and CTA; anonymous character never shows facial identity; risks/limitations and human reviewer are named. Any missing required field or face reveal is a fail requiring remediation.
