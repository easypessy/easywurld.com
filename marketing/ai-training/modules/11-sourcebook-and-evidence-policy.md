# 11 — Sourcebook & evidence policy

**Owner:** Research lead / curriculum editor · **Prerequisites:** None; use throughout · **Pass evidence:** Citation audit and source update record

## How to use this sourcebook

This curriculum is a synthesis and a set of working rules, not an empirical study. The references below provide foundational concepts, method guidance and official policy starting points. They do **not** prove that a tactic will work for Easywurld or any specific client. Check the edition, current availability, jurisdiction, audience and method before relying on a source. Paywalled books are listed as reading recommendations, not free resources.

## Evidence labels

- **Established finding:** repeated, well-supported evidence within a defined research context; still has limits and may not transport to a new audience.
- **Method/policy guidance:** authoritative instructions or framework, applicable only within its stated scope and date.
- **Practitioner model:** useful organizing framework, not a universal law or causal guarantee.
- **Creative hypothesis:** idea to test; not evidence until studied in context.

For each external factual claim, cite an accessible primary/authoritative source where possible, record access date, describe population/method, quote accurately and link the exact claim. Prefer systematic reviews/meta-analyses for broad conclusions, transparent empirical research for effects, and the originating authority for rules. If evidence conflicts, state the conflict rather than select the most convenient result.

## Core reading and references

### Marketing, brand and strategy

- Kotler, Philip & Kevin Lane Keller. *Marketing Management* (15th edition, Pearson, 2016). Broad strategy and marketing-management text; textbook framework, not campaign-specific evidence.
- Sharp, Byron. *How Brands Grow* (Oxford University Press, 2010); and Sharp et al., *How Brands Grow Part 2* (OUP, 2016). Evidence-informed brand growth and buyer behavior perspective. Treat specific generalizations with attention to category, market and evidence base.
- Ehrenberg-Bass Institute for Marketing Science, research and resources: https://www.marketingscience.info/ . Practitioner-facing summaries should be traced to the underlying work where a material claim is used.
- American Marketing Association, definitions and professional resources: https://www.ama.org/ . Use as an orientation resource, not empirical proof for a campaign claim.

### Consumer behavior and ethical influence

- Kahneman, Daniel. *Thinking, Fast and Slow* (Farrar, Straus and Giroux, 2011). Influential synthesis; some cited literatures have since been debated or revised. Do not use as a plug-and-play copywriting formula.
- Cialdini, Robert B. *Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion*, revised editions (Harper Business). Practitioner synthesis of social influence; apply ethically and evaluate fit.
- Thaler, Richard H. & Cass R. Sunstein. *Nudge: The Final Edition* (Yale University Press, 2021). Choice architecture framework; transparency and user welfare remain essential.
- Fogg, B. J. *A Behavior Model for Persuasive Design* (2009), DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/1541948.1541999 . A design model for analyzing behavior; not a guarantee of conversion.
- National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), *AI Risk Management Framework 1.0* (2023): https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework . Risk governance framework; voluntary and not a substitute for law.

### Writing, accessibility and storytelling

- Heath, Chip & Dan Heath. *Made to Stick* (Random House, 2007). Practical message-design model; practitioner framework, not a universal causal formula.
- Nielsen Norman Group, writing/usability research and guidance: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ . Guidance varies by task and user; validate with target users where feasible.
- W3C, Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2: https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/ . Technical accessibility standard; check applicable conformance obligations and the current version.

### Research and experimentation

- American Association for Public Opinion Research (AAPOR), transparency initiative and best practices: https://aapor.org/standards-and-ethics/ . Useful for transparent survey reporting; not all campaign research is a survey.
- UK Government Analysis Function, evaluation and experimental design guidance: https://analysisfunction.civilservice.gov.uk/ . Methods resources; adapt carefully to commercial and local contexts.
- Google Analytics documentation: https://support.google.com/analytics/ . Definitions and product setup change; use only the current documentation for the account configuration.
- Platform-native analytics documentation (Meta, TikTok, YouTube, LinkedIn, etc.) should be located and date-checked at project kickoff; platform-defined metrics are not interchangeable.

### Advertising, privacy, rights and synthetic media

- Nigerian Data Protection Commission: https://ndpc.gov.ng/ . Start with official current Nigerian data protection resources; obtain qualified advice for application.
- Advertising Regulatory Council of Nigeria (ARCON): https://arcon.gov.ng/ . Verify current applicable advertising rules and approval requirements with qualified counsel/agency compliance.
- Federal Trade Commission, endorsements and advertising guidance: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing . U.S.-specific; useful comparative reading only, not Nigerian legal advice.
- UK Information Commissioner's Office, AI and data protection guidance: https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/artificial-intelligence/ . UK-specific; not a substitute for Nigerian or other applicable law.
- YouTube altered/synthetic content disclosure guidance: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491 . Platform-specific and subject to change; check current policy and applicability before upload.
- TikTok synthetic media policy/help centre: https://support.tiktok.com/ . Find the current official AI-generated content disclosure rules; do not rely on reposts or old screenshots.
- U.S. Copyright Office, AI initiative: https://www.copyright.gov/ai/ . U.S.-specific reports and resources; copyright treatment varies by jurisdiction and continues to develop.

## Source quality checklist

For each source ask: Who created it and why? Is it primary/secondary? What method, population and date? What does it actually support? What are its limitations/conflicts of interest? Is the setting comparable? Is the policy still current? Can the result be independently checked? What evidence could disconfirm our interpretation?

## Citation and AI research rules

- Never cite a tool's answer as evidence; cite the original source it actually supports.
- Open every link, verify the title and relevant passage, and retain a source copy or exact access details where licensing permits.
- Do not invent references, quotations, authors, statistics or page numbers. Mark unavailable sources and remove unsupported statements.
- Distinguish a source's words from your interpretation; quote sparingly and accurately.
- Record when AI helped discover or summarize sources and have a human independently inspect the actual source.
- Keep a versioned bibliography and note broken links or superseded guidance during quarterly review.
