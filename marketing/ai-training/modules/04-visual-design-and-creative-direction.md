# 04 — Visual design & creative direction

**Owner:** Art direction / design · **Prerequisites:** 00–03 · **Pass evidence:** Approved visual concept, specifications and accessibility review

## What this department must know

Design shapes attention, comprehension, feeling and recognition through choices about hierarchy, composition, type, colour, image, motion and consistency. Good taste is valuable but not a substitute for audience fit, legibility, brand rules or technical checks.

### Fundamentals

- **Visual hierarchy:** establish entry point, order, grouping, contrast and a single primary action. Use size, position, weight, colour and whitespace intentionally.
- **Composition:** balance, alignment, rhythm, scale, negative space, focal length/crop and visual flow; consider how crop changes meaning.
- **Typography:** choose for legibility, tone, language coverage, weight, line length and screen context. Protect minimum usable sizes; test on actual mobile previews.
- **Colour:** use approved brand tokens and semantic role, not arbitrary decoration. Check contrast and meaning across contexts; colour must not be the only way to communicate state.
- **Images and visual metaphor:** choose images for relevance, not stereotype. Avoid visual claims the copy does not support. Maintain provenance and license records.
- **Motion:** motion should explain, guide or express; respect reduced-motion preferences and avoid animation that blocks reading.
- **Design systems:** reusable tokens, type scale, spacing, logo clear space, safe zones and export specs. A system enables consistency without making every piece identical.
- **Accessibility:** contrast, meaningful reading order, alt text for informational images, captions/transcripts for audiovisual work, and layouts that tolerate zoom and different screens.

## Easywurld direction baseline

Use beige `#FBE7C5`, deep green `#144D3C`, white and black as supporting colours unless the approved brand owner updates the kit. The character is anonymous, human-realistic silhouette only; no facial identity, including in reflective surfaces. Keep continuity of clothing, proportions, movement and light. No unrelated logos or invented client marks. The visual system should feel intelligent, direct, creative, calm, confident and honest—not generically “AI futuristic.”

For client work, obtain the client-approved current brand system; never infer that the Easywurld palette or anonymous figure belongs to them. If source files conflict with this baseline, stop and request brand-owner resolution.

## Creative direction brief

- Strategic objective, priority audience and one intended audience response.
- Central idea and why it fits the insight, offer and brand.
- Visual metaphor / subject / setting and emotional temperature.
- Composition, palette, lighting, camera relation, type and motion language.
- Required/forbidden content; character continuity and anonymity rules.
- Channel aspect ratio, safe zones, duration or static dimensions; platform spec check date.
- Caption, alt-text and contrast/readability plan.
- Asset references with provenance, license and rationale (not “copy this artist”).
- Variations to test, what remains constant and what is allowed to change.
- Review/approval and final export checklist.

## Creative review: five questions

1. Can the audience identify the subject and the idea quickly?
2. Does each visual choice support the message and brand, rather than merely decorate?
3. Are claims, people, places, product and context represented honestly?
4. Can a viewer read, understand and access the essential meaning?
5. Is the idea distinctive without copying another creator or confusing the brand?

## Connections

Strategy defines meaning; psychology informs cognitive load and decision cues; copy sets hierarchy and proof; design establishes the visual system; cinematography converts direction into moving images; synthetic-media identity rules govern generated people; distribution dictates format adaptation; governance checks provenance, permissions and accessibility.

## Assessment

Produce two distinct static concept boards and a design rationale from the same strategy brief. Include palette/type/hierarchy, crop, asset provenance, alt text and mobile preview. **Pass:** both concepts express the same strategic idea with different executions; the brand identity is correct; no identity reveal or unsupported visual implication; copy is legible and accessible; source/rights are documented. **Remediate:** revise any palette conflict, stereotype, inaccessible hierarchy or unlicensed reference before approval.
