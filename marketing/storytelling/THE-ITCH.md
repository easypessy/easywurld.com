# THE ITCH

**Easywurld's storytelling system for short-form.**
The structure that turns a scroll into a watch, a watch into a follow, a follow into a message.

---

## 0. The one idea

> **Make an itch in the first four seconds. Don't scratch it until the last three.**

Everything below is that sentence, made mechanical.

A viewer does not leave a video because it is bad. They leave because **nothing is unfinished**. A finished thought is a reason to scroll. An unfinished one is a reason to stay.

So the whole craft is: open something they need closed, then make the closing worth the wait.

---

## 1. The three jobs (one post does one)

This is the part most accounts get wrong, and it is why 37 posts a day can still produce nothing.

| Tier | Job | What it buys | Share of posts |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ENTERTAIN** | Make them watch to the end, and share it | **Reach** — strangers | **60%** |
| **TEACH** | Make them trust that you know the thing | **Trust** — followers who save | **30%** |
| **SELL** | Make it easy for the ready ones to buy | **Money** | **10%** |

**A single post cannot do all three.** The moment a post tries to entertain *and* teach *and* sell, it becomes an advertisement with a joke in front of it, and the viewer feels the hand on their shoulder.

So: pick **one job per post**. Then let the three tiers feed each other:

```
ENTERTAIN ──reach──▶ strangers see you
    │
    └──▶ TEACH ──trust──▶ they follow, they save, they remember
             │
             └──▶ SELL ──ease──▶ the ready 10% pay
                      │
                      └──▶ they tell someone ──▶ back to ENTERTAIN
```

The entertainment tier is not the "fun" one you do when you have time. It is the **entire top of the funnel**. It is the only thing that reaches people who have never heard of you. Without it, teaching and selling only speak to the people who already know you — which, at the start, is almost nobody.

> **Evidence:** Adley Kinsman (Viralish, 1B+ organic views a month) puts it bluntly: there is a place for middle- and bottom-of-funnel content, "but 60% plus of your content or more should be top-of-funnel content." Top-of-funnel is the content made for people who are not yet customers. [Source](https://www.socialmediaexaminer.com/6-steps-formula-to-a-billion-views-adley-kinsman-viral-video-secrets/)

## 2. THE ITCH — the volume format (15–30 seconds)

Four beats. The acronym is the order.

```
I  IMAGE   0.0 – 1.5s   A picture that does not belong
T  TRAP    1.5 – 4.0s   The belief they hold and shouldn't
C  COST    4.0 – 9.0s   What it is costing, in a number or a moment
H  HOW     9.0 – 27s    The turn: show the fix, beat by beat
   SCRATCH 27 – 30s     Close the loop. Then open the next one.
```

### I — IMAGE (0.0–1.5s)

The first frame must break a pattern. The brain predicts "talking head on a phone" and scrolls past it. Give it something it did not predict.

**What to film instead of your face:**
- The thing itself, wrong: a printed homepage on a plate of jollof. A price list taped to a wall.
- A screen, held close, at an angle, mid-scroll.
- Your hand doing something: closing a laptop, drawing a line through a page.
- A number, huge, on paper.

**Rules**
- No logo in the first frame. Ever. A logo is a promise of an ad.
- No "hey guys", no "welcome back", no "in this video". These are all reasons to leave.
- No slow zoom on a static image — that is the visual signature of "more of the same".
- If you can, start **mid-motion**: the sentence already half-spoken, the page already half-scrolled.

### T — TRAP (1.5–4.0s)

The itch itself. One sentence that names a belief the viewer is holding right now, and quietly tells them it is wrong.

**Four shapes that work:**
1. **The correction** — "Your website isn't ugly. It's unclear. Those are different problems."
2. **The confession** — "We turned down ₦180,000 last month."
3. **The combo** — two things that don't belong together, held in one frame (a rack of ribs on a car engine works because it raises a question the eye can't answer).
4. **The stake** — "This one sentence is why your WhatsApp enquiries go cold."

**The test:** cover the rest of the video. Does the first line leave a question open? If you can answer it from the line itself, it is a statement, not a hook.

### C — COST (4.0–9.0s)

Nothing is interesting without a price. The cost is what turns "that's true" into "that's happening to me".

Make it **specific and local**: naira, a day of the week, a customer's actual words, a Lagos street, a time of night.

- Weak: "It can cost you customers."
- Strong: "Twelve enquiries last month. Four replied. Two bought. You paid for all twelve."

The cost is also where **stakes** live, and stakes are why anyone cares. Same idea, filmed in a market, is worth more than the same idea filmed at a desk — because something feels at risk.

### H — HOW (9.0–27s)

The turn. This is where the teaching lives — but it arrives *after* they are already watching, never before.

**Two rules for the middle:**

1. **But / Therefore between every beat.** If two beats are joined by "and then", cut one. "We launched the site, **but** nobody called. **Therefore** we rewrote the first screen." That is a story. "We launched the site, and then we rewrote it" is a list of events, and lists of events are why people scroll.

2. **Change something every 3–4 seconds.** A new shot, a new number on screen, a cut to the thing being described. The eye needs a reason to stay in the frame. Not effects — *change*.

**Length:** the middle should be **70% of the runtime**. If your hook is 4 seconds and your payoff is 4 seconds, that leaves 22 seconds of middle on a 30-second video. Most people write a 30-second video as 2 seconds of hook and 28 seconds of payoff, and wonder why retention dies at 8 seconds.

### SCRATCH (27–30s)

Close the loop you opened. One line. Then point at the next one.

- **Scratch:** "It was never ugly. It was unclear. Fix the first sentence before the font."
- **Loop:** "Tomorrow I'll show you the same problem on a restaurant page."

The loop matters because **rewatches and shares are counted**, and because the next video now has an audience that already wants it. Ending well is how you get a series, and a series is how you get a following.

**Where the offer goes:** at the end of *sell* posts only. On entertain and teach posts, the door is the bio link and one soft line — "the full list is on the site" — never a pitch. The itch is the product; don't sell at the moment of scratching.

## 3. THE STORY SPINE — the flagship (45–90 seconds)

Use this once or twice a week for the post you want remembered. It is the Pixar spine, adapted to a business, with one extra beat at the end that does the teaching.

```
1. There's a business that …        (the world as it is)
2. Every day they …                 (the ordinary day)
3. Until one day …                  (the break)
4. Because of that …                (consequence)
5. Because of that …                (consequence, worse or better)
6. Until finally …                  (the turn)
7. And that's the part you can steal …   ← the teach beat
```

**Beat 7 is the whole point.** It lets you entertain for 80% of the video and still teach — without the teaching feeling like a bait-and-switch, because the viewer *wants* the lesson by then. The story earns the lesson.

**Why this structure specifically:** the "because of that" chain is a *therefore* chain (cause and effect), which is what separates a story from a list of events. The Pixar spine came from improv teacher Kenn Adams and bakes cause-and-effect into every step. [Source](https://thescientistvideographer.com/wordpress/the-pixar-storytelling-formula/)

**A note on whose story it is:** the customer is the hero. Easywurld enters at beat 6 ("until finally, someone rewrote the first screen"). If you make yourself the hero, it becomes a case study; if you make them the hero, it becomes a story. Case studies are saved. Stories are shared.

## 4. THE HONEST AD — the sell format (20–40 seconds)

Selling without hype, because hype is what Easywurld is positioned against. Five beats.

```
1. A real, specific, mildly funny problem      (entertain)
2. "Here's what nobody tells you"             (itch)
3. Show the thing — one page, one screen      (proof)
4. Say the price AND one honest limit         (trust)
5. One action, one word                        (ease)
```

**Beat 4 is the differentiator.** "It's ₦35,000 for all nine. It won't get you customers — it gives you the scripts for the customers you already get." Nobody else selling a ₦5,000 product says that. That sentence is worth more than the whole ad, because it proves you are not lying about the other parts.

**The "allow whoever wants to buy" mechanics** — the user's own phrase, made concrete:

| Mechanic | Rule |
|---|---|
| The link | Always the same place (bio / one pinned comment). Never spoken in the video. |
| The ask | One word. "BUNDLE." A code, not a call to action. |
| The reply | Whoever comments gets a DM within the hour. Replies are the conversion. |
| The pressure | None. No countdowns, no "only 3 left". They will not believe it and it costs you the trust the teach tier built. |
| The path | Three taps from the video to a payment. If it is more, that is the leak to fix first. |

## 5. The quality gate — run every script through this

Five checks. A script that fails two is not ready.

**1. The 3-second test.** Does frame one break a pattern? Is the first line a question left open?

**2. The mute test.** Turn the sound off. Do the captions carry the whole story? Most of Lagos watches on mute in a shared space. If the video only works with sound, half your audience gets nothing.

**3. The but/therefore test.** Write the beats as one line each. Put a connector between each pair. If you write "and then" more than once, rewrite the middle.

**4. The 70% test.** Is the middle actually 70% of the runtime? (Hook 0–4s, middle to ~26s, scratch 3s.)

**5. The stranger test.** Show it to someone who does not know your business. Ask one question: *"Would you keep watching?"* The second they say they would scroll, that beat is the problem. Not the whole script — that beat.

There is a tool for checks 1, 3 and 4: `npm run story -- path/to/script.md`. See section 9.

## 6. Text on screen — the rules for a Lagos phone

- **One idea per frame.** Six words is a lot. If a caption needs two lines, it needs two frames.
- **Bottom third is dead.** The caption bar and the action buttons live there.
- **Top 10% is dead.** The status bar and the "For You" label.
- **Keep text inside the middle 80%.**
- **Contrast, not colour.** Cream `#FAE7C7` background, deep green `#0A4937` type — the brand palette is already the highest-contrast pairing you have. Use it and nothing else.
- **Never put the logo over the story.** Mark at the end card, small, bottom right, with clear space.
- **Burned-in captions, always.** Not the auto ones — they lose your punchlines and mistype "Easywurld".

Brand rules stay as they are in [`../brand-prompt.md`](../brand-prompt.md). The system here changes the *structure*, not the look.

## 7. The hook bank — 24 openers, ready to use

Steal these, then rewrite them in your own words. An unedited hook is someone else's voice.

**Correction (they believe a false thing)**
1. "Your website isn't ugly. It's unclear."
2. "You don't have a lead problem. You have a follow-up problem."
3. "Nobody scrolls past your prices because they're high. They scroll because they can't find them."
4. "Your logo is not the reason nobody is buying."
5. "That post didn't fail because the algorithm hates you."

**Confession (something you shouldn't say)**
6. "We turned down ₦180,000 last month."
7. "We told a client to cancel the project."
8. "This is the part of our work we don't charge for."
9. "I've written 300 posts this month. Nine of them worked."
10. "Our own site was slow for three weeks and I knew it."

**Combo (two things that don't belong together)**
11. "Here's a restaurant menu printed on a CV."
12. "This is a ₦40,000 website in a ₦400,000 business."
13. "I put a customer's WhatsApp in a spreadsheet. Watch what happens."
14. "This business answers 40 messages a day with four sentences."

**Stake (something is about to be lost)**
15. "This one sentence is why your enquiries go cold."
16. "Read the last line of your homepage. That's why."
17. "You are paying for traffic that lands on this."
18. "Twelve people asked you for a price last month. How many did you follow up?"

**Demonstration (show, don't claim)**
19. "I'm going to fix this page in 30 seconds. Count with me."
20. "Two versions of the same headline. One of them gets ignored."
21. "This is everything a Lagos business needs to sell on WhatsApp. It fits on one page."
22. "Watch what happens when I delete the top half of this homepage."

**Question (they answer in the comments — which is the point)**
23. "What's the first thing you'd change here?"
24. "Which one would you trust? Be honest."

## 8. Worked examples

### Example A — FIX THIS (enterprise version of THE ITCH, 28s)

> **I (0:00)** — Screen recording, thumb scrolling a bakery's homepage. The headline on screen: *"Welcome to our website."*
> **T (0:02)** — "This bakery is not bad. This website is."
> **C (0:05)** — "It says 'welcome' where it should say what they sell. So a stranger lands, learns nothing in four seconds, and leaves. They baked for someone who never came in."
> **H (0:10)** — "Here's the fix. One line. **But** don't write 'quality bread' — every bakery writes that. **Therefore** write the thing only they can say: *'Bread out of the oven at 6am, Agege.'* **But** that's still not enough — **therefore** the button underneath says *'Order for pickup'*, not 'Contact us'."
> **SCRATCH (0:24)** — "A stranger now knows what you sell, when it's ready, and what to do. That's it. That's the whole page."
> **LOOP (0:27)** — "Tomorrow: the same problem, but on a food vendor's WhatsApp status."

**Why it works:** the "but/therefore" chain is visible in the script itself. Fourteen seconds of middle, four of hook, three of payoff. Nothing sells.

### Example B — WE SAID NO (story spine, 62s)

> **1 (0:00)** — "There's a restaurant in Lekki that wanted a new website."
> **2 (0:05)** — "Every day they posted on Instagram, answered forty DMs, and took orders by voice note. The food was good. The queue on Fridays was real."
> **3 (0:12)** — "Until one day they asked us for a full rebuild. ₦180,000. They were ready to pay that day."
> **4 (0:20)** — "Because of that, we opened their site. And it was… fine. Slow, ugly, but it loaded, it had prices, and the WhatsApp button worked."
> **5 (0:30)** — "Because of that, we asked one question: how many people who message you actually order? They said about one in five."
> **6 (0:38)** — "Until finally we told them to keep the website and spend the money on the reply. Twenty minutes of scripts, a follow-up sheet, one person answering DMs before 9am."
> **7 (0:50)** — "Their site is still ugly. Their orders went up. **And that's the part you can steal:** before you pay for a new website, count how many people already message you and never get a reply."
> **SCRATCH (0:58)** — "We would rather tell you not to spend. It's better business."

**Why it works:** this is the entertainment tier doing trust work without trying. It cannot be faked — which is exactly why it is worth making. Nobody else in Lagos is posting this.

### Example C — THE STEAL (teach, 26s)

> **I (0:00)** — A phone showing a WhatsApp chat, scrolling.
> **T (0:02)** — "Every price reply in Nigeria makes the same mistake. It ends with a question."
> **C (0:05)** — "'How much is it?' — '₦15,000. Do you want it?' That question hands the decision back to a person who was already deciding. Half of them go quiet, and you think they lost interest. They didn't. You asked."
> **H (0:12)** — "Say the price, say what happens next, say nothing else. **But** add the one thing they're about to ask — delivery — before they ask it. **Therefore:** '₦15,000. Delivered Wednesday. Send your address and I'll reserve one.'"
> **SCRATCH (0:22)** — "One sentence. No question mark. That's the whole change."
> **LOOP (0:25)** — "The other four replies are in the pack on the site."

### Example D — THE HONEST AD (bundle, 34s)

> **1 (0:00)** — Nine printed documents fanned on a table, shot from above.
> **2 (0:03)** — "These are all the things we sell separately. Today I'm going to tell you not to buy most of them."
> **3 (0:08)** — "Nine packs. Scripts, spreadsheets, a homepage blueprint. ₦5,000 each — that's ₦45,000 if you take them one at a time."
> **4 (0:16)** — "**But** nobody needs all nine on day one. **Therefore** the bundle is ₦35,000, and the honest limit is this: it will not get you a single customer. It gives you the scripts for the customers you already get."
> **5 (0:28)** — "If you already get messages you don't reply to in time, start there. One word on WhatsApp: BUNDLE."

## 9. The tool

`npm run story -- path/to/script.md` checks a draft against this system:

```bash
npm run story:example
```

It flags:
- an opening line that is too long, or that opens with a banned phrase ("hey guys", "in this video", "welcome back")
- a middle that is under 60% of the runtime
- more than one "and then" (no cause and effect)
- no "but" / "therefore" connector at all
- a missing payoff or missing loop
- word count against the target duration

It cannot judge whether it is *good*. It can tell you whether it is *built correctly*, which is the part you can check at 11pm when you are tired and about to post something flat.

## 10. How to use this without burning out

1. **Pick one format per post before you write a word.** Entertain, teach, or sell. Write it at the top of the script file.
2. **Batch hooks, not videos.** Write 20 hooks in one sitting. Hooks are 90% of the outcome and 10% of the effort. Adley Kinsman's own process: write four different hooks for the same idea and shoot all of them, then test.
3. **Shoot in one block.** One afternoon, one outfit, one location, six videos.
4. **Keep the format, change the subject.** Big creators post in one to three formats, endlessly. Novelty is expensive; consistency compounds.
5. **Let the scorecard decide, not your mood.** The number that matters is conversations per 10 posts — see [`../measurement/README.md`](../measurement/README.md).

---

## 11. What this system is not

- It is not a way to go viral. Virality is a by-product of retention, and retention is a by-product of a viewer wanting the next thing.
- It is not a licence to be dishonest. Every beat above assumes the story is real. A fabricated "we said no" is worse than no story, and it is the one thing you cannot take back.
- It is not finished. It is a first version — add formats, break rules deliberately, and keep the two that work.
