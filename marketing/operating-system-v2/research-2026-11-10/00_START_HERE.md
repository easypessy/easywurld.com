## The five findings that actually change how the drafts can be written

1. **Nigerian consumer law is stricter than "don't lie".** FCCPA s.123(1)(d) puts the *burden of proof* on the
   seller for any performance or efficacy statement. So "this doubled enquiries" is not a bold claim, it is an
   unevidenced one. Same for turnaround promises.
2. **A published testimonial needs the real words and written permission** (FCCPA s.126). No paraphrase, no
   "a client once said". This kills any invented quote in Series 7.
3. **A displayed price is binding, and the lowest displayed price wins** (FCCPA s.115). "From ₦X" copy in Series 10
   is a legal commitment, not a hook.
4. **Meta's own rules tell you they are not the law.** Its standards require compliance with local law *and*
   self-regulatory advertising codes on top, review is automated, and ads can be re-reviewed and reversed
   (`PL-META-03`). Platform approval is not a Nigerian clearance.
5. **Data-protection registration can bite a small business.** The NDPC's threshold is **more than 200 data
   subjects in six months**, or operating in listed sectors including health, education, hospitality, financial
   and e-commerce (`NG-NDPA-10`). A small clinic or school can qualify. This is the single most useful fact in the
   register for the audience, and it was nowhere in the drafts before.

## Honest limits

This is a **snapshot dated 2026-11-10**, not legal advice and not a compliance conclusion. Three source classes
