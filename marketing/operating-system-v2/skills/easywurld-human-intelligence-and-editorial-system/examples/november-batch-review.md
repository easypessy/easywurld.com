# Worked review — 14–30 November structured first-pass copy

**Scope:** `marketing/november-2026-content-drafts/output/` is an existing draft set, not verified customer evidence. This example illustrates how to use the skill before approving publication; it does not approve any of the 391 later-date posts.

**BLUF: REVISE / HOLD.** Each of the 17 days has the requested 12 + 3 + 5 + 3 text slots and a manually chosen separate 12th buyer problem. But many series repeat the same six-step scaffold, repeated “a buyer asks...” opening and generic next-step sentences. Unique niche names and unique hooks do not establish naturalness, usefulness or G2 editorial quality. No content has been typeset into exported art or tested in an actual composer.

- **A/B — Experience:** The fictional 14 November grocer buyer asks whether an item is in stock today. This is an editorial scenario, *not* a customer report. Do not infer that a customer was frustrated or that an owner spent time answering DMs.
- **C — Shared pattern:** The hypothetical tailor, bookshop and grocer all raise a “confirm current availability before effort” question; they differ in stock volatility, appointment constraints and reservation commitments. Connection is low-confidence until actual enquiries are observed. Do not turn 17 sectors into one asserted insight.
- **D — Root-cause alternatives:** A grocer's repeated stock question might come from stale page copy, unavailable inventory data, an unreliable update owner, or a buyer needing same-hour certainty. Ask the operator how stock is maintained and review consented buyer questions before choosing which cause to address.
- **E — Solutions:** Immediate: provide an owner-approved private stock check route. Process: timestamp a maintained stock list with an update owner. Strategic: only if the process is stable and evidence warrants it, integrate inventory; do not automate an unreliable list. Measure how often a buyer can get an accurate answer and how often an old stock claim needs correction, against a real baseline.
- **F — Editorial correction:** Original first pass: “Why does ‘Is the listed item in stock today?’ still need a DM?” This is a writer's diagnostic, not the buyer's most natural voice. Test a more concrete, explicitly hypothetical opening: “The shelf photo shows the item. Is it still there today?” Follow with *how* the store can confirm stock and who maintains the answer; do not say stock is available. Vary the opening, evidence and action of nearby S02–S04 rather than replacing the niche noun in the same frame.
