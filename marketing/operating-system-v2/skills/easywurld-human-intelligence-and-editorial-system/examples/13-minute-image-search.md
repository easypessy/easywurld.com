# Worked example — image search interrupts a post (illustrative, not a customer interview)

**Status:** Hypothesis. The quoted 13 minutes is part of the supplied scenario, not independently observed evidence. No case study, performance result or publication decision follows from it.

- **A — Experience:** An owner intended to draft one post, looked for an appropriate food/apple picture for 13 minutes, found one and reported losing the original message and motivation. We do not know how often this occurs, whether they own suitable images or whether another task interrupted them.
- **B — Sensation:** Reported: prolonged search and lost momentum (only if the owner actually said this). Inference: the task felt more effortful than expected, because the image search delayed writing. Unverified: embarrassment, exhaustion and belief that posting is pointless; ask rather than asserting.
- **C — Shared mechanism:** A person hunting for an application document may experience setup effort before beginning the core task. Similarity: searching consumes the attention reserved for action. Difference: an application may have higher stakes and privacy needs; medium confidence as a possible transfer, low as a general behavioural claim. A shopper comparing unclear product options may also stop; difference: that is decision uncertainty rather than asset retrieval, so confidence is low until investigated.
- **D — Symptom and alternatives:** Symptom: a post stalls after image selection. Candidate causes: assets have no tags; the post has no clear message; approval uncertainty creates avoidable browsing; the owner may simply have a higher-priority interruption. Distinguish by observing several consented drafting sessions and where the delay actually occurs. One anecdote does not prove an asset-library problem.
- **E — Options:** Immediate: use a permissioned, relevant existing visual or draft the text first; costs almost nothing, risk of irrelevant imagery. Process: name and tag a small approved visual library with usage rights and an owner; costs maintenance time. Strategic: simplify content briefs, editorial decisions and approvals *before* buying automated asset search; requires team agreement. Recommend the immediate test plus a tiny library if repeated sessions confirm search is the bottleneck. Baseline: time from intent to first usable draft and quality/fit score for several real posts; observe over a defined window, without promising a conversion lift.
- **F — Sample communication:** “You sit down to post a helpful idea. Thirteen minutes later, you've found a picture but lost the sentence you meant to write. Sometimes the obstacle isn't motivation; it's a missing starting point. Try writing the point first, then choose from a small folder of approved images. If the same step keeps slowing you down, label that folder by topic and rights.” This is illustrative copy; do not present it as a testimonial.
