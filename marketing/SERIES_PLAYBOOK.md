# Easywurld series playbook

**Active campaign:** 2 November–1 December 2026
**Cadence:** 10 core posts per day, adapted to TikTok, Instagram and Facebook = 30 placements per day.

This playbook turns the strategy into repeatable production rules. The canonical editorial source is `marketing/series-plan.json`. The expanded machine-readable plan is `marketing/content-plan.json`.

## 2. Series rules

### If I ran this account

- Say “If I ran” or “I would test.” Do not imply Easywurld manages the business.
- Give three post ideas, one mistake and one first action.
- Use Lagos/Nigeria context where relevant, but do not invent local facts.
- Label hypothetical businesses, prices and chats as examples.

### Marketing Myth Court

- Use a verdict: true, mostly false, partly true, false, or it depends.
- Do not replace one absolute with another.
- Give a small test the owner can run.
- Do not claim platform rules without a current source.

### Trend Desk

Trend posts are **held** until the verification log is complete.

1. Check the dated source already attached to the brief.
2. Within seven days before publishing, find a current public example on the target platform.
3. Record the example URL, platform, date checked, rights check, relevance note and reviewer in `marketing/TREND_VERIFICATION.csv`.
4. If the format is still relevant and rights are clear, update the script to say “this week” or “last week” only when the evidence supports it.
5. If no current example is verified, publish only as a dated teardown: “Reported on [date]. Here is the structure.” Remove live-trend wording.

Late-October 2026 candidate formats include Brand Vibe, Pick Your Emoji, Gold Star Behavior, short proof Reels, native-interface tutorials and comment-keyword DM flows. These are candidates, not guaranteed November trends. ### AI Automation Build

- Show one workflow: input → AI draft → human check → output.
- State what is not automated.
- Use anonymised or fictional input.
- Keep prices, promises, legal/medical/financial answers and complaints behind human review.
- If using a repo demo, label it concept/sample. Do not imply it sent real messages or produced live results.

### Memes

- Two per day: one owner-life, one marketing/AI/client.
- Original text-led graphics or short text videos.
- Joke about the work, not the customer.
- No fake notifications, stolen templates, private screenshots or punching down.
- The caption carries the useful lesson.

### Offer Spotlight A and B

- One featured offer per day.
- Post A: problem + actual contents + price + DIY boundary.
- Post B: one objection or limitation + direct answer + same keyword CTA.
- Rotate the nine packs. Do not sell all nine in one day.
- Confirm price, contents, payment and delivery before publishing.
- No guaranteed results, fake scarcity or invented testimonials.

### Steal This Copy Move

- Attribute the principle to the expert and source.
- Steal the structure, not long copyrighted copy.
- Use an original Easywurld/Lagos example.
- Do not imply the expert endorses Easywurld.

Rotation: David Ogilvy, Eugene Schwartz, Joe Sugarman, Gary Halbert, Claude Hopkins, John Caples, Joanna Wiebe, Harry Dry, Ann Handley, Robert Bly.

Reference points: Ogilvy on research and headlines; Schwartz on existing desire and awareness; Sugarman on reading momentum; Halbert on market and offer; Hopkins on testing; Caples on self-interest headlines; Wiebe on voice-of-customer; Dry on concrete, checkable copy; Handley on utility and voice; Bly on benefits and clear CTAs. ### Meet Easywurld

- A new angle and format each day.
- Rotate: studio map, one-sentence studio, process, sample labels, service menu, FAQ, AI policy, handover, pricing boundary, brief template, contact route, audience map, sample tour, series map, website anatomy, follow-up flow, local checklist, proposal anatomy, message worksheet, launch checklist, tool stack, behind the calendar, homepage tour, boundaries, who we serve, how to start, review rhythm, invitation, dictionary.
- Do not invent team size, office, clients or results.
- Do not use the fictional demo founder as the owner.

## 4. Platform adaptation

## 5. Weekly batch rhythm

Each week produces 70 core posts and 210 platform placements.

| Block | Weekly output | Notes |
|---|---:|---|
| Niche plans | 7 carousels | Batch by niche; use owned/labelled visuals |
| Myth Court | 7 talking heads | Record in one avatar/voice session |
| Trend Desk | 7 teardowns | Research first; hold until verified |
| AI Build | 7 workflows | Record real demos or set workflow cards |
| Memes | 14 originals | Design from one template system |
| Offer | 14 posts | One pack, two angles per day |
| Copy Move | 7 breakdowns | Attribute principle, write original example |
| Meet Easywurld | 7 intros | Rotate format and angle |

Suggested weekly timing:

- **Sunday:** verify trends, approve scripts, batch record/edit, design statics.
- **Daily:** assemble the 10 core posts, adapt captions, QA, schedule only approved assets.
- **Saturday/Sunday:** reply, review series metrics, prepare next batch.

This is a heavy cadence. Use templates, batch generation and a shared asset library. If a slot fails QA, hold it rather than publishing unreviewed AI. Do not stack catch-up posts into a spam burst.

## 6. QA gate per placement

Before a placement moves from Draft to Scheduled:

- [ ] Facts, price, offer and sample labels checked.
- [ ] Trend verification complete, or live-trend wording removed.
- [ ] Approved likeness/voice or non-avatar fallback.
- [ ] Captions, pronunciation, crop and disclosure checked.
- [ ] One CTA and destination tested.
- [ ] Watched/read on a phone; human approval recorded.

Statuses: Draft → Script approved → Asset produced → QA approved → Scheduled → Published → Reviewed.

## 7. Measurement

Review by series after seven days:

- Saves/shares for teaching and copy posts.
- Watch time/completion for talking heads and demos.
- Profile visits and WhatsApp keyword enquiries.
- Qualified project briefs and confirmed pack sales.
- Meme shares without attracting the wrong audience.

Keep the 10/day test, but change hooks, formats and angles based on evidence. Do not declare a winner from one post. Do not invent month-end results.
