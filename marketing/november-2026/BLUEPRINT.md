# The carousel blueprint

The template behind all ten November posts. Use it to write December yourself,
or to brief anyone who writes for you.

---

## 1. The shape that works (7 slides)

| Slide | Job | Never |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Hook** | One feeling. Under twelve words. No logo clutter, no "swipe →". | Explain, hedge, or introduce yourself |
| **2 — Pain** | The customer's own words for the problem. Make them think *"that's me"*. | Be reasonable or polite |
| **3–6 — Point** | One idea per slide, each with a boring, specific example | Two ideas, abstract advice, stock photos |
| **7 — Send + step** | Ask for the share, then one small action | Ask for a sale, or give five links |

Five-slide version for status sequences: hook → the one idea → the proof →
the flip → the ask.

### Why this order

Slide 1 buys attention with an uncomfortable truth. Slide 2 converts attention
into recognition. Slides 3–6 pay the reader back so the share is deserved.
Slide 7 spends the goodwill — and you only get to spend it if the previous six
slides were worth something.

---

## 2. Hook formulas (tested shapes, not magic)

Copy one, change the nouns.

| Formula | Example used | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Callout** | "Your website is why they picked your competitor." | Accuses gently; the reader argues, then reads |
| **Audit** | "I opened 20 Lagos business sites. 17 had the same problem." | Specific numbers read as evidence |
| **Cost** | "Your website is losing you customers every day. Here's the leak." | Names a loss already happening |
| **10-second test** | "Can a stranger tell what you sell in ten seconds?" | Turns the reader into the test subject |
| **Before/after** | "Same business. Same products. Two homepages." | Promises a visible difference |
| **Myth-break** | "You don't need a bigger website. You need a clearer one." | Contradiction creates the scroll |
| **Reply bait** | "Drop your link. I'll tell you the first thing I'd fix." | Gives a reason to comment, not just read |
| **Confession** | "I almost quit in month three." | Personal stakes are the rarest thing on LinkedIn |

**Test rule:** same body, three different hooks. Post variant A, wait 48 hours,
post variant B to a different platform, compare **saves and shares**, not likes.

## 3. The emotional layer (what makes them feel it)

Facts make a carousel useful. Feeling makes it travel. Three moves:

1. **Name the private moment, not the general problem.**
   Not "websites need clear pricing." → "They never called, so you assumed they
   weren't interested."
2. **Use the customer's exact words.** "DM for price." "This is too simple."
   "So what do you do exactly?" Quotes do the heavy lifting.
3. **Admit the cost to you.** "I argued. Then I opened it on my phone." A writer
   who was wrong is trusted faster than one who is always right.

Storytelling spine for the personal carousels:
**ordinary scene → the mistake → the moment of evidence → what it cost → the rule
you kept.** Five beats, seven slides, no moralising at the end.

---

## 4. Copy rules

- **Say the number.** ₦45,000, twenty guests, ten seconds, 9am. Vagueness is the
  fastest way to sound like everyone else.
- **One sentence per line.** Three-line paragraphs are where readers leave.
- **Ban these words:** unlock, elevate, seamless, cutting-edge, game-changer,
  revolutionary, "in today's fast-paced world", "let's dive in".
- **Never invent proof.** No client names, no "we increased sales by 300%", no
  logos you didn't work for.
- **Write the slide, then delete a third.** If removing a sentence doesn't hurt,
  it was decoration.
- **Read it out loud.** If you wouldn't say it to a shop owner in Lekki, rewrite
  it.

---

## 5. Caption structure per platform

| Platform | Length | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| **LinkedIn** | 120–220 words | Line 1 repeats the hook (it's truncated after ~2 lines). Then short lines, one idea each, ending with a specific ask. 3–5 hashtags at the bottom, never in the middle. |
| **Instagram** | 60–100 words | Hook, three short beats, "save this", CTA. 8 hashtags, all relevant. |
| **TikTok / Reels** | 20–40 words | Timestamped beats for the voiceover plus the on-screen text. The caption is the plan, not the script. |
| **WhatsApp status** | 40–80 words | Written like a message to one person. No hashtags, no emoji spam. Ends with "message CLEAR". |

## 6. Design rules

- Cream `#FAE7C7` background, deep green `#0A4937` headline, one terracotta
  accent, ink body. Same typefaces as the site.
- The hook slide is mostly empty. That emptiness is the design.
- Text sits inside an 92 px margin; the footer (EASYWURD · slogan · slide
  number) never moves, so all ten posts look like one campaign.
- Art is drawn in code (browser, phone, clock, before/after, seal) — no AI
  imagery, so nothing contradicts the copy.
- 1080×1350 feed, 1080×1920 status.

## 7. The prompt template (for you or any AI)

```
You are writing slide [N] of a 7-slide Easywurld carousel.

Audience: Lagos small-business owners who have a website or are about to pay
for one. They are practical, sceptical of hype, and have been disappointed by
an agency before.

Voice: plain, specific, confident, occasionally blunt. Nigerian business
English, no pidgin unless the hook needs it. No jargon.

Post hook: "[PASTE HOOK]"
This slide's job: [hook / pain / point / proof / share / CTA]
This slide's one idea: [ONE SENTENCE]

Rules:
- Under 22 words for the headline, under 45 for the body.
- One idea. No lists unless the idea is a list.
- Include one boring, specific example (a price, a time, a place, a quote).
- No invented statistics, client names or results.
- Do not use: unlock, elevate, seamless, game-changer, dive in.
- End the slide with the sentence the reader would repeat to a friend.

Return: headline, body, and a one-line art description.
```

---

## 8. Before you publish — the 6-point check

1. Does slide 1 make me feel something in three seconds?
2. Is the example specific enough that a competitor couldn't have written it?
3. Is there exactly one idea per slide?
4. Would I send this to someone? (If no, fix slide 7, not slide 3.)
5. Is every number true?
6. Does the caption repeat the hook in its first line?

---

## 9. How to break this blueprint

Rules are a floor, not a ceiling. Break these on purpose, never by accident:

- **No hook slide.** Open on the proof (the screenshot, the receipt). Works when
  the proof is genuinely surprising.
- **One slide.** A single sharp page outperforms seven weak ones. If the idea
  fits in one, post one.
- **No CTA.** Ends on the feeling. Only works if slide 7 is quotable on its own.
- **Ten slides.** LinkedIn allows 20; longer works when each slide is a beat in
  a story, not a bullet.
- **No numbers.** Feelings can carry a post — but then every word has to earn it.
- **Two ideas.** Allowed only if they argue with each other (myth vs reality).

The test for breaking a rule: can you say why it will work *for this post*, in
one sentence? If not, you're guessing.
