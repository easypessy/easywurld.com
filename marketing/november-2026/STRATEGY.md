# The strategy behind the 30 days

The record of why this works the way it does. Read it once; revisit it on day 30
when you decide what to keep.

---

## 1. What this is actually for

Not reach. Not likes. **Enquiries you can answer with a price.**

Every post is built to move someone one step along a single path:

```
see something true (TikTok / Reels)
        ↓
save it or send it (Instagram / Facebook)
        ↓
check who made it (profile, pinned Easywurld post)
        ↓
message CLEAR (WhatsApp)
        ↓
free audit → the door (site or copy) → retainer → systems
```

If a post does not move someone along that path, it is decoration — even if it
performs.

---

## 2. Why fifteen touches and not one good post

Each platform does a different job, and the jobs are not interchangeable:

| Platform | Job | What "working" looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **TikTok** (4/day) | Reach strangers who have never heard of you | New profile views, not likes |
| **Instagram** (4/day) | Make the idea portable — saves and sends | Saves per reach |
| **Facebook** (3/day) | Convince the older, price-aware local buyer | Messages, shares into groups |
| **Stories** (3/day) | Stay in front of people who already know you | Poll votes, sticker taps, DMs |
| **LinkedIn** (1/day) | Authority with people who have budget and a title | Comments from owners, not other designers |

Fifteen sounds like a lot. In practice it is **eleven assets**, each published to
its first platform at the right hour and cross-posted with a rewritten first
line. The heavy work is the writing, and that is already done per day.

The honest guardrail: if the volume starts costing quality, cut **TikTok to two
and Facebook to two**. Never cut the Easywurld slot or the proof slot — those two
are what turn an audience into clients.

---

## 3. The four-week arc

A month needs a shape, or it becomes thirty unrelated posts.

| Week | Theme | What it does to the audience | What it asks for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Clarity | "I think my site might be the problem" | Nothing yet — recognition only |
| **2** | Price & perception | "I could be charging better / showing a price" | A comment or a save |
| **3** | Proof | "These people actually do this work" | A link or a sentence sent to you |
| **4** | Story & offer | "I know who they are, and what it costs" | The message |

Weeks run alongside the weekday themes: Monday clarity, Tuesday price,
**Wednesday proof + Easywurld**, Thursday story, Friday argument, Saturday Lagos,
Sunday reflection. Two rhythms — weekly and monthly — so the feed never feels
like a loop.

## 4. The eleven slots, as a funnel

| Slot | Sells | By |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Diagnose | Attention | Proving you can see their problem in ten seconds |
| 2 Teach | The copywriting door | Giving away a method that works without you |
| 3 Personal story | Trust | Being a person with a record of being wrong |
| 4 Proof | Credibility | Screenshots, labelled honestly |
| 5 Status lesson | Reach | Twenty seconds of one idea, three times a day |
| 6 Opinion | Positioning | Disagreeing with a belief they hold |
| 7 Belief | Price | Standards that explain why you are not the cheapest |
| 8 Status proof | Warmth | The work, in a story they can tap |
| 9 Invitation | Conversation | Doing a small piece of work for free, in public |
| 10 Behind the scenes | Likeability | The unromantic truth of running a studio |
| 11 Easywurld | Conversion | Answering "who are you, what do you cost, what do I own" |

Ratio: **5 business · 3 personal · 2 status · 1 studio.** Five arguing, three
confessing, two reminding, one introducing. Any month that is all business reads
like a brochure; all personal reads like a diary. The ratio is the product.

---

## 5. Why the examples rotate (angle × sector)

`plan.mjs` pairs each slot's angle with a different sector every day: bakery,
fashion, auto, clinic, school, property, restaurant, salon, consultant, logistics.

Two reasons:

1. **A follower never hears the same sentence twice.** Ten angles across ten
   sectors is a hundred variations before anything repeats.
2. **A stranger sees their own business in your example.** The clinic owner does
   not care about bakeries; on day 6 the example is a clinic.

This is also why the writing cannot be fully templated: the sector changes the
price, the objection and the photograph. The plan fixes *what* each post argues;
the copy file fixes *how*.

## 6. The testing protocol

1. **Three hooks, one body.** Post the same carousel with three different first
   slides across three weeks, on the same platform, same hour.
2. **Judge on saves and shares.** Likes are a vanity signal; saves mean "I will
   need this", shares mean "someone I know needs this".
3. **Two-week rule.** After fourteen days, rank all eleven slots by saves +
   shares + DMs. Cut the bottom two and double the top one. Do not negotiate
   with the data.
4. **One experiment at a time.** Changing the hook, the time and the format in
   the same week teaches you nothing.
5. **Keep a wins file.** Screenshot every comment that says "this is me". Those
   are your next five hooks, written by the audience.

---

## 7. Production, so it actually ships

| Step | When | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Write eleven angles | Sunday morning | 90 min (already written for the built days) |
| Render slides | one command | 5 s |
| Read every caption once, adjust voice | Sunday | 20 min |
| Schedule the week | Sunday evening | 30 min |
| Reply to comments | daily, first 30 min after each post | 15 min |
| Review the numbers | Sunday, 15 min | 15 min |

Rules that protect the week: **batch on Sunday**, **never post and leave**, and
**one idea per asset**. If a carousel needs a second idea, it is two posts.

---

## 8. Honesty rules (non-negotiable)

- No invented clients, revenue, rankings or testimonials.
- Concept work is labelled **concept build** on the slide itself.
- "I opened 20 sites" only if you opened twenty.
- Personal stories use your real memory; the framework is reusable, the facts
  are not.
- No promise of results you cannot control — rankings, virality, revenue.

This is not morality for its own sake. A small studio's only durable asset is
being believed, and one fabricated number destroys it faster than a year of good
posts builds it.

---

## 9. From comment to client

The content's job ends at a message. This is what happens next:

1. **Reply within the hour** — a question, not a pitch. "What do you sell, and
   who buys it?"
2. **Send the free audit** — three things, in order, with the cost of each.
   One page. No logo, no deck.
3. **Offer the door only** — the site or the copy, whichever the audit points
   at. Never both, never the retainer.
4. **At handover, the one-pager** — "three more things we noticed". That is the
   ladder, sold with evidence instead of enthusiasm.
5. **Ask for the referral at the moment of approval**, not at the invoice.

---

## 10. Day 30: what to look at

Bring these four numbers per slot: **saves, shares, DMs, follows**.

Questions for the review:
1. Which slot produced the most DMs? That slot gets more days.
2. Which hook formula won? Use it for the next three weeks.
3. Which platform produced clients, not just reach? Weight it.
4. Which sector's examples got the strongest response? That is your niche talking.
5. Did the Easywurld post produce profile visits? If not, the story is too vague —
   name the place, the year, the first client.
6. What did clients actually say when they messaged? Those words become next
   month's hooks.

Then rebuild `plan.mjs` with the winners, and run the next thirty days with a
narrower argument and a louder example.

---

## 11. What would make me change this plan

- If three weeks pass with saves but no messages, the offer is not visible
  enough — move the Easywurld slot to twice a week.
- If messages come but no one can afford the door, the audience is wrong —
  shift the sectors toward property, clinics and schools.
- If a single post out-performs everything, stop the grid for a week and make
  five variations of it. The plan serves the post, not the other way round.
