## LinkedIn

A business owner told me last week that her website "doesn't bring customers."

I asked what happened when people visited. She didn't know. Nobody ever told her.

Here is the quiet thing about a website: people rarely complain. They just leave. No message, no call, no explanation. You only see the silence, so you assume there was no interest.

Usually there was interest. It died in the gap between "this looks nice" and "I trust these people enough to send money."

Four things close that gap, and none of them is a redesign:

1. Show the thing you sell clearly, in a photo taken with intention.
2. Put the price next to what the price includes. A number alone always feels expensive.
3. Move your best work out of WhatsApp and onto the page.
4. Give people one obvious next step. One button. Not five.

If you own a business in Lagos, try this tonight: open your site on your phone, outside, on mobile data, and read the first screen as a stranger. If you can't tell what you sell and what it costs in ten seconds, that's your leak.

## Slide copy (for reference / edits)

1. **Your website is why they picked your competitor.**
2. **They found you. They looked. They left.** — And because they never called, you assumed they weren't interested. They were. They just didn't trust what they saw.
3. **Trust is decided before they read a word.** — One bakery shows twelve phone-shot photos and no prices. Another shows three clear ones, a delivery note and a real address. Same cake. Same price.
4. **A price with no reason is just a number.** — ₦45,000 on its own reads expensive. ₦45,000, feeds twenty, delivered across Lekki by 9am — that reads fair.
5. **Your best work is buried in WhatsApp.** — The photos customers ask for every week should be the first thing a stranger sees. Not something you send privately at 11pm.
6. **Then there is no next step.** — If the only way to reach you is a number at the very bottom, most people won't look for it. One clear button fixes more than a full redesign.
