## LinkedIn

A price on its own is not information — it is a warning.

"₦2.5m." reads as expensive, because the reader's mind fills the silence with the worst story it knows: hidden costs, a bad street, a landlord who disappears.

Four pieces turn a number into a decision:

1. The unit — "per annum", "per plot", "per 20 guests".
2. The inclusions — water, security, parking. Each one removes a question.
3. The anchor — what comparable things cost. Context, not a discount.
4. The next step — one line, one button.

₦2.5m per annum, 3-bedroom serviced, includes water and security, similar units on this street run ₦2.8–3m. Message for a viewing.

Same price. Now it reads as fair, and the wrong buyers filter themselves out before they waste your Saturday.

If you sell anything with a number attached — property, cakes, courses, retainers — rewrite one line tonight with those four pieces.

## Slide copy (for reference / edits)

1. **Never write a price on its own.**
2. **A number with no reason reads as a warning.** — ₦2.5m. That is all. The buyer's mind fills the gap with the worst story it knows.
3. **“₦2.5m per annum, 3-bedroom, serviced.”** — Now it is not a price. It is a category. The reader either belongs here or does not — both outcomes are useful.
4. **“Includes water, security, and parking for two.”** — Every inclusion removes one reason to ask a question you do not have time to answer.
5. **“Comparable units on this street run ₦2.8m–3m.”** — An anchor is not a discount. It is context. It tells the buyer you know the market, which is the whole job of a listing.
6. **Rewrite one line: price, unit, inclusions, anchor.** — Four pieces, one sentence. It takes three minutes and it changes who calls you. *Send it to CLEAR on WhatsApp and I'll tighten it.*
