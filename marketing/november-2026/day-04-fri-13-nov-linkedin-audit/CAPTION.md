## LinkedIn

I opened 20 Lagos business websites last month. 17 had the same problem — and it wasn't the design.

None of them was ugly. Most looked tidy. Some were genuinely beautiful. That is exactly why the owners never fixed them.

What was missing, in order of how often I saw it:

1. No clear next step. No order button, no brief form, no "book a call". Just a number in the footer.
2. Prices missing, or hidden behind "DM for price". The owner thinks that protects them; the buyer reads it as "expensive, or negotiable in a way that will waste my time".
3. Proof replaced by logos. A wall of company logos nobody recognises, and no photos of real work.
4. Slow on mobile data. Fine on office wifi, painful on a three-year-old phone in traffic — which is how almost everyone will meet the site.

The fix for all four is usually an afternoon's work: a headline that says what you sell, one honest photo, a price range or a clear "from ₦__", and a single button.

## Slide copy (for reference / edits)

1. **I opened 20 Lagos business sites last month. 17 had the same problem.**
2. **None of them was ugly.** — That is the trap. Owners look at their site, decide it looks fine, and stop asking the only question that matters.
3. **There was no clear next step.** — No “order here”. No “send your brief”. Just a phone number in the footer and an Instagram icon. People do not hunt for the door.
4. **Prices were missing — or meaningless.** — “DM for price” feels safe to the owner and suspicious to the buyer. If you can't publish it, publish what it includes.
5. **The proof was a logo wall.** — Ten logos nobody recognises, no photos of the actual work. Show one real job, described honestly. That beats twenty logos.
6. **Half of them were slow on mobile data.** — Not on office wifi. On data, in traffic, on a phone that is three years old. That is how your customer is actually meeting you.
7. **Most of it is an afternoon, not a rebuild.** — A clearer headline. One honest photo. A price range. One button. *Message CLEAR on WhatsApp for a five-minute review of yours.*
